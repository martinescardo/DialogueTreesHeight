\title[Dialogue trees of system T]{The dialogue trees of system T have
  height below $\varepsilon_0$, and this bound is sharp}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
  The dialogue interpretation of G\"odel's system T assigns to each
  closed term of type $(\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ a well founded tree,
  branching over the natural numbers, whose nodes record the queries
  the term makes to its oracle. We prove that the height of this tree,
  measured as a Brouwer ordinal code, is below~$\eps$, and that
  $\eps$ is the least such bound, a closed term attaining the height
  $\tower n$ for every~$n$. The upper bound assigns to every term,
  by induction on its type, a majorant built from addition, suprema
  and suprema of iterates, and bounds the majorant by a tuple of codes
  graded by the order of the type, in which an entry is raised only by
  entries of its own order or higher. The lower bound runs Church
  encodings of the approximants of~$\eps$ against the oracle. The
  development is formalized in Agda, with function extensionality as
  its only assumption. Xiao and Sun have proved the same bound, with
  a computable tower level for each term, over classical ordinals.
  The proof offered here is an alternative one, constructive, and it
  settles the sharpness question that they leave open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

G\"odel's system T defines only continuous functionals
$\Baire\to\N$. One proof of this, by effectful forcing, interprets
each closed term $t$ of type $(\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ as a dialogue
tree~\cite{Escardo2013}: a well founded tree, branching over the
natural numbers, whose nodes carry the queries the term makes to its
oracle and whose leaves carry its answers. Continuity is read off the
tree, since a leaf is reached after finitely many queries. The trees
are Church encoded within system T itself
in~\cite{EscardoPaivaRahliTosun2025}.

The tree is well founded, so it has an ordinal height, and the
question is how large that height can be. The answer is that the
height is below~$\eps$, and that no smaller ordinal bounds the heights
of all the trees.

The first of these was proved by Xiao and Sun~\cite{XiaoSun2026}, who
compute from each term a tower level below~$\eps$ bounding the height
of its tree, by translating recursors into closed infinitary templates
and removing redexes in a sequence of passes indexed by type level.
Their ordinals are the classical ones, and their formalization assumes
excluded middle for the propositions of the second universe, along
with univalence, propositional truncation and set replacement. They
write that they do not claim the bound sharp, and they leave open
whether the heights are cofinal in~$\eps$.

What this paper adds is twofold. The proof is constructive: the
ordinals are Brouwer codes with their natural order, so the whole
development assumes function extensionality and nothing else, and,
classically, a bound in that order implies the classical one. And the
heights are
shown to be cofinal in~$\eps$ (\cref{cor:least}), which answers the
question Xiao and Sun leave open and makes $\eps$ the least bound.
What this paper does not supply is their computable tower level: the
bound obtained here is $\eps$ itself, with no finite level named for a
given term.

Two features of the problem shape the proof. The first is that the
height of the tree of a term is not a function of the values the term
computes. A term whose numerical output is small may ask the oracle a
number of questions that no bound on its output predicts, so an
ordinal assignment bounding the values of a term, such as Howard's
\cite{Howard1970}, does not by itself bound the height of its dialogue
tree. The second is that the recursor at higher types iterates a
functional rather than a number, and the bound for the iterates has to
be hereditary in the type.

We meet the first by bounding the height directly, with a logical
relation over the types of system T whose ground case reads
\[
  \height(d) \le a ,
\]
for a dialogue tree $d$ and a Brouwer code~$a$. We meet the second by
making the majorant hereditary, with a pointwise supremum and a
pointwise translation at every type, so that the recursor acquires a
majorant at every type and not only at the ground type. This reduces
the theorem to a statement of ordinal arithmetic
(\cref{thm:reduction}): the majorant of a closed term of ground type
is below~$\eps$.

A majorant is a functional over Brouwer codes, built from addition
and from suprema, among them the suprema of iterates. To bound one by
an ordinal below~$\eps$ we bound it by a tuple of codes graded by the
order of the type: a
translation at order~$0$, an exponent at order~$1$, the exponent of
that exponent at order~$2$, and so on. One rule governs the whole
construction, that an entry is raised only by entries of its own order
or higher, which is the rule that iterating $x\mapsto\omega^{x}$
breaks. The maps between such tuples that obey the rule form a
calculus with application, abstraction, projection and iteration
(\cref{sec:graded}), and the two hard clauses of the induction on
terms, the one for~$\Sc$ and the one for the recursor, are then
written as a reader of $\lambda f\,g\,a.\,f\,a\,(g\,a)$ would write
them.

For the lower bound we write Church encodings of the approximants
of~$\eps$ as terms of system T, after Coquand, Hancock and
Setzer~\cite{CoquandHancockSetzer1997}, and run them against the
oracle through an algebra that places one query above the tree built
so far. A parametricity argument shows that the resulting tree has the
intended height.

\section{Dialogue trees and their heights}
\label{sec:dialogue}

We recall the dialogue trees of~\cite{Escardo2013}, the functor and
monad they form with the Kleisli extension and the generic oracle, and
the dialogue interpretation that assigns a tree to each closed term of
system T.

\begin{definition}[Dialogue trees]
\label{def:dialogue-tree-type}
For a type~$X$, the type $\Dial X$ of dialogue trees over~$X$ is
inductively generated by
\[
  \eta : X \to \Dial X , \qquad
  \beta : (\N \to \Dial X) \to \N \to \Dial X .
\]
A tree $\beta\,\varphi\,i$ asks the oracle for its value at~$i$ and
continues with $\varphi\,j$ when the answer is~$j$, and a tree $\eta\,x$
answers~$x$. Running a tree against an oracle $\alpha : \Baire$ gives
\[
  \mathrm{dialogue} : \Dial X \to \Baire \to X ,
\]
defined by $\mathrm{dialogue}\,(\eta\,x)\,\alpha = x$ and
$\mathrm{dialogue}\,(\beta\,\varphi\,i)\,\alpha =
 \mathrm{dialogue}\,(\varphi\,(\alpha\,i))\,\alpha$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{EffectfulForcing.MFPSAndVariations.Dialogue}{D};
\texttt{B}, \texttt{dialogue}.}
\end{definition}

The operation $\Dial$ is a functor, the tree $\Dial f\,d$ being $d$
with its leaves relabelled by~$f$, and a monad with unit~$\eta$.

\begin{definition}[Kleisli extension and the generic oracle]
\label{def:kleisli-generic}
The Kleisli extension of $g : X \to \Dial Y$ is the map
$\kext g : \Dial X \to \Dial Y$ that grafts the tree $g\,x$ onto each
leaf $\eta\,x$,
\[
  \kext g\,(\eta\,x) = g\,x , \qquad
  \kext g\,(\beta\,\varphi\,i) = \beta\,(\kext g \circ \varphi)\,i .
\]
The generic oracle is the map
$\mathrm{generic} = \kext{(\beta\,\eta)} : \Dial\N \to \Dial\N$, where
$\beta\,\eta$ sends~$i$ to the tree $\beta\,\eta\,i$, which asks the
oracle at~$i$ and answers what it is told. Thus
\[
  \mathrm{generic}\,(\eta\,i) = \beta\,\eta\,i , \qquad
  \mathrm{generic}\,(\beta\,\varphi\,i)
    = \beta\,(\mathrm{generic}\circ\varphi)\,i ,
\]
and $\mathrm{generic}\,d$ asks the oracle at the value that the tree
$d$ computes,
\[
  \mathrm{dialogue}\,(\mathrm{generic}\,d)\,\alpha
  = \alpha\,(\mathrm{dialogue}\,d\,\alpha) .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{EffectfulForcing.MFPSAndVariations.Dialogue}{kleisli-extension};
\texttt{generic}, \texttt{generic-diagram}, \texttt{B-functor}.}
\end{definition}

We write $\iterop$ for iteration on the natural numbers,
$\iterop\,f\,x\,0 = x$ and
$\iterop\,f\,x\,(n+1) = f\,(\iterop\,f\,x\,n)$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{EffectfulForcing.MFPSAndVariations.Combinators}{iter}.}

\begin{definition}[The dialogue interpretation]
\label{def:dialogue-interpretation}
Write $\Tm\sigma$ for the closed terms of the combinatory system T,
with constants $\Zeroc$, $\Succc$, $\Iterc$, $\Kc$ and~$\Sc$, and
$\TmO\sigma$ for the same language extended with a constant $\Omega$
of type $\gr\arr\gr$ for the oracle. The dialogue interpretation of a
type is
\[
  \dsem{\gr} = \Dial\N , \qquad
  \dsem{\sigma\arr\tau} = \dsem{\sigma} \to \dsem{\tau} ,
\]
and the dialogue interpretation of a closed term $t$ of $\TmO\sigma$
is an element $\dsem{t}$ of~$\dsem\sigma$, the same brackets serving
for both because the argument says which is meant. It interprets
$\Omega$ as $\mathrm{generic}$, $\Zeroc$ as $\eta\,0$, $\Succc$ as
$\Dial$ applied to the successor, $\Kc$ and~$\Sc$ as the projection
and the substitution, and the recursor $\Iterc$ at type~$\sigma$ as
\[
  \iterop'\,f\,x = \kext{(\iterop\,f\,x)} ,
\]
the Kleisli extension taken at type~$\sigma$. The dialogue tree of a
closed term $t : (\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ is
$\dtree(t) = \dsem{\overline t \cdot \Omega}$, where $\overline t$ is
the image of~$t$ in~$\TmO$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{EffectfulForcing.MFPSAndVariations.MFPS-XXIX}{B⟦\_⟧};
\texttt{B-Set⟦\_⟧}, \texttt{Kleisli-extension}, \texttt{iter'},
\texttt{dialogue-tree};
\Agda{EffectfulForcing.MFPSAndVariations.CombinatoryT}{T},
\texttt{TΩ}, \texttt{embedding}.}
\end{definition}

The Kleisli extension in that definition is taken at an arbitrary
type, which asks for more than the monad supplies. For
$g : \N \to \dsem\sigma$ and a tree $d$, define $\kext g\,d$ in
$\dsem\sigma$ by recursion on the type: at the ground type it is the
Kleisli extension of the monad, and at an arrow it acts under the
argument,
\[
  (\kext g\,d)\,s = \kext{(k \mapsto g\,k\,s)}\,d .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{EffectfulForcing.MFPSAndVariations.MFPS-XXIX}{Kleisli-extension}.}

The constant $\Iterc$ iterates a function rather than performing
primitive recursion, and we call it the recursor throughout. We also
abbreviate combinatory terms by $\lambda$-notation, as in
$\lambda f.\,f\,0$ for $\Sc\cdot I\cdot(\Kc\cdot\Zeroc)$, where
$I = \Sc\cdot\Kc\cdot\Kc$ is the identity, the abbreviation standing
for the term that bracket abstraction produces.

The height of a dialogue tree is defined in the next section, once the
Brouwer codes are at hand.

\section{Brouwer codes and their arithmetic}
\label{sec:codes}

We take the ordinals to be Brouwer codes with their natural order,
for which the successor is monotone by construction, and prove the
properties of the order; we define addition, multiplication,
exponentiation and the tower whose supremum is~$\eps$ and prove their
laws; we prove the closure properties of~$\eps$ and the laws of
absorption that the proof uses; and we define the height of a dialogue
tree with its three properties.

A classical treatment would interpret the height of a dialogue tree as
an ordinal in the sense of the univalent library, and then the grafting
and generic lemmas need excluded middle, because the successor map on
ordinals is not monotone without it. Brouwer codes with their
natural order avoid this.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{Ordinals.AdditionProperties}{succ-not-necessarily-monotone}.}

\begin{definition}[Brouwer codes and their order]
\label{def:codes}
The type $\Brouwer$ of Brouwer ordinal codes is inductively generated
by
\[
  \bZ : \Brouwer , \qquad
  \bS : \Brouwer \to \Brouwer , \qquad
  \bL : (\N \to \Brouwer) \to \Brouwer .
\]
The natural order $\le$ on~$\Brouwer$ is inductively generated by four
rules,
the least element property, the successor rule, the cocone and the
limit rule, saying that $\bZ$ is least, that the successor is
monotone, that $\bL f$ is above each $f\,n$, and that $\bL f$ is below
every upper bound of the family~$f$:
\[
  \frac{\ }{\bZ \le b} \qquad
  \frac{a \le b}{\bS a \le \bS b} \qquad
  \frac{a \le f\,n}{a \le \bL f} \qquad
  \frac{f\,n \le b \ \text{for every $n$}}{\bL f \le b} .
\]
We write $a < b$ for $\bS a \le b$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{Ordinals.BrouwerCodes}{B};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Order}{\_≤\_},
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{\_<\_}.}
\end{definition}

A limit is therefore the least upper bound of its family, and the
successor is monotone for arbitrary codes, which is what the classical
reading lacks. Identities between codes are proved below by induction,
and at a limit they use function extensionality, to identify the
limits of two families that agree pointwise.

\begin{lemma}[The order]
\label{lem:order}
Let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be codes and $f$ and $g$ families of codes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:refl}
  $a \le a$.
\item\label{item:trans}
  If $a \le b$ and $b \le c$ then $a \le c$.
\item\label{item:S-incr}
  $a \le \bS a$.
\item\label{item:L-mono}
  $f\,n \le \bL f$ for every~$n$, and if $f\,n \le g\,n$ for every~$n$
  then $\bL f \le \bL g$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Order}{≤-refl}; \texttt{≤-trans},
\texttt{≤-L-mono}, \texttt{≤-L-upper-bound};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{S-increasing}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(\labelcref{item:refl}) By induction on~$a$: $\bZ \le \bZ$ by the least
element property, $\bS a \le \bS a$ by the successor rule, and
$\bL f \le \bL f$ by the limit rule, each $f\,n$ being below $\bL f$ by
the cocone applied to $f\,n \le f\,n$.

(\labelcref{item:trans}) By recursion on the two derivations, by cases
on the last rule of the first and then of the second. If the first is
the least element property, then $a = \bZ$ and $\bZ \le c$. If the
first is the limit rule, with $a = \bL f$ and $f\,n \le b$ for
every~$n$, we recurse on each $f\,n \le b$ with the same second
derivation and apply the limit rule. If the first is the successor
rule, from $a' \le b'$, the second ends either with the successor rule,
from $b' \le c'$, and we recurse on the two premises, or with the
cocone, from $\bS b' \le g\,m$, and we recurse on the first derivation
and that premise and apply the cocone. If the first is the cocone, from
$a \le f\,n$ with $b = \bL f$, the second ends either with the cocone,
from $\bL f \le g\,m$, and we recurse on the first derivation and that
premise and apply the cocone, or with the limit rule, from
$f\,k \le c$ for every~$k$, and we recurse on $a \le f\,n$ and
$f\,n \le c$. No other combination of rules can occur, since the least
element property has $\bZ$ on the left, the successor rule successors
on both sides, the cocone a limit on the right and the limit rule a
limit on the left. Each recursive call has a smaller first derivation,
or the same first derivation and a smaller second one, so the recursion
terminates.

(\labelcref{item:S-incr}) By induction on~$a$: $\bZ \le \bS\bZ$ by the
least element property, $\bS a \le \bS\bS a$ by the successor rule,
and for a limit $f\,n \le \bS(f\,n) \le \bS\bL f$, by induction, by
the successor rule applied to the cocone $f\,n \le \bL f$, and
by~(\labelcref{item:trans}), so that the limit rule applies.

(\labelcref{item:L-mono}) The first claim is the cocone applied
to~(\labelcref{item:refl}). For the second, $f\,n \le g\,n \le \bL g$
by the cocone, and the limit rule applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:one-branch}
Two asymmetries of the order govern what can be proved about it. A
lower bound $a \le \bL f$ holds as soon as $a \le f\,n$ for a single
branch~$n$, while an upper bound $\bL f \le b$ asks for every member of
the family. A numeral therefore fits under one branch of a tree, which
is what makes a lower bound of~$\omega$ available by induction
(\cref{prop:omega-attained}), and a limit does not, which is why the
lower bounds of~\cref{sec:sharp} go through terms rather than through
the order alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Irreflexivity]
\label{lem:irrefl}
For no code~$a$ is $\bS a \le a$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Examples}{S-irrefl}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on~$a$, by the last rule of a derivation of
$\bS a \le a$. No rule concludes $\bS\bZ \le \bZ$, for the reason
given in the proof of~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:trans}). A
derivation of $\bS\bS a \le \bS a$ ends with the successor rule, from
$\bS a \le a$, which the induction hypothesis excludes. A derivation of
$\bS\bL f \le \bL f$ ends with the cocone, from $\bS\bL f \le f\,n$
for some~$n$. Then $\bS(f\,n) \le \bS\bL f \le f\,n$, by the successor
rule applied to the cocone $f\,n \le \bL f$ and by transitivity, which
the induction hypothesis at $f\,n$ excludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Arithmetic of codes]
\label{def:arithmetic}
Addition, multiplication and base~$\omega$ exponentiation are defined
by recursion on the right argument, respectively on the exponent:
\begin{align*}
  a \bplus \bZ    &= a ,                      &
  a \btimes \bZ   &= \bZ ,                    &
  \omega^{\bZ}    &= \bS\,\bZ ,               \\
  a \bplus \bS b  &= \bS (a \bplus b) ,       &
  a \btimes \bS b &= (a \btimes b) \bplus a , &
  \omega^{\bS a}  &= \omega^{a} \btimes \omega , \\
  a \bplus \bL f  &= \bL (n \mapsto a \bplus f\,n) , &
  a \btimes \bL f &= \bL (n \mapsto a \btimes f\,n) , &
  \omega^{\bL f}  &= \bL (n \mapsto \omega^{f\,n}) .
\end{align*}
The numerals are $\num 0 = \bZ$ and $\num{n+1} = \bS\,\num n$, the
limit $\omega$ is $\bL(n\mapsto\num n)$, the tower is
$\tower 0 = \omega$ and $\tower{n+1} = \omega^{\tower n}$, and
\[
  \eps = \bL(n \mapsto \tower n) .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Order}{\_⊕\_};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Multiplication}{ι[\_]}, \texttt{ω}, \texttt{\_⊗\_};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{ω\^{}\_}, \texttt{tower}, \texttt{ε₀}.}
\end{definition}

The equation $a \bplus \bS b = \bS(a \bplus b)$ makes addition on the
left commute with successors and limits definitionally, which is what
the inductions below rely on.

\begin{lemma}[Addition]
\label{lem:add}
Let $a$, $a'$, $b$ and $c$ be codes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:Z-unit}
  $\bZ \bplus a = a$.
\item\label{item:add-incr}
  $a \le a \bplus b$ and $b \le a \bplus b$.
\item\label{item:add-mono}
  If $a \le a'$ then $a \bplus c \le a' \bplus c$, and if $b \le c$ then
  $a \bplus b \le a \bplus c$.
\item\label{item:add-assoc}
  $(a \bplus b) \bplus c = a \bplus (b \bplus c)$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{Z-left-unit};
\texttt{⊕-increasing-right}, \texttt{⊕-mono-right};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Multiplication}{⊕-increasing-left},
\texttt{⊕-mono-left}, \texttt{⊕-assoc}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part~(\labelcref{item:Z-unit}) is an induction on~$a$, and
(\labelcref{item:add-assoc}) an induction on~$c$. In
(\labelcref{item:add-incr}), $a \le a \bplus b$ is an induction
on~$b$: at a successor $a \le a \bplus b \le \bS(a \bplus b)$
by~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:S-incr}), and at a limit,
$a \le a \bplus f\,0$ by induction, and $a \bplus f\,0$ is below
$\bL(n \mapsto a \bplus f\,n)$ by the cocone. The inequality $b \le a \bplus b$ is an induction on~$b$, by the
least element property, the successor rule
and~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:L-mono}). In
(\labelcref{item:add-mono}), monotonicity on the left is an induction
on~$c$, by the successor rule and~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:L-mono}).
Monotonicity on the right is an induction on the derivation of
$b \le c$. For the least element property,
$a \bplus \bZ = a \le a \bplus c$ by~(\labelcref{item:add-incr}), and
each of the other three rules is applied again to the sums, since
$a \bplus \bS b = \bS(a \bplus b)$ and
$a \bplus \bL f = \bL(n \mapsto a \bplus f\,n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Multiplication]
\label{lem:mult}
Let $a$, $a'$, $b$, $c$, $x$ and $y$ be codes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:one-unit}
  $\num 1 \btimes y = y$ and $x \btimes \num 1 = x$.
\item\label{item:mult-mono}
  If $a \le a'$ then $a \btimes c \le a' \btimes c$, and if $b \le c$
  then $a \btimes b \le a \btimes c$.
\item\label{item:distrib}
  $c \btimes (x \bplus y) = c \btimes x \bplus c \btimes y$.
\item\label{item:mult-assoc}
  $(a \btimes b) \btimes c = a \btimes (b \btimes c)$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{SZ-⊗}; \texttt{⊗-mono-left},
\texttt{⊗-mono-right};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Multiplication}{⊗-left-distrib},
\texttt{⊗-assoc}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(\labelcref{item:one-unit}) The first identity is an induction on~$y$,
since $\num 1 \btimes \bS y = \num 1 \btimes y \bplus \num 1
= \bS(\num 1 \btimes y)$, and the second is
$x \btimes \num 1 = \bZ \bplus x = x$, by~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:Z-unit}).

(\labelcref{item:mult-mono}) Monotonicity on the left is an induction
on~$c$: $a \btimes \bS c = a \btimes c \bplus a \le a' \btimes c \bplus
a \le a' \btimes c \bplus a'$ by~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:add-mono})
twice, and limits are handled
by~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:L-mono}). Monotonicity on the right
is an induction on the derivation of $b \le c$: the least element
property gives $\bZ \le a \btimes c$, the successor rule gives
$a \btimes b \bplus a \le a \btimes c \bplus a$
by~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:add-mono}), and the cocone and the
limit rule are applied again to the products, since
$a \btimes \bL f = \bL(n \mapsto a \btimes f\,n)$.

(\labelcref{item:distrib}) By induction on~$y$, the case of a successor
being
$c \btimes (x \bplus \bS y) = c \btimes (x \bplus y) \bplus c
= (c \btimes x \bplus c \btimes y) \bplus c
= c \btimes x \bplus (c \btimes y \bplus c)$
by~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:add-assoc}).

(\labelcref{item:mult-assoc}) By induction on~$c$, the case of a
successor being
$(a \btimes b) \btimes \bS c = a \btimes (b \btimes c) \bplus a \btimes b
= a \btimes (b \btimes c \bplus b) = a \btimes (b \btimes \bS c)$
by~(\labelcref{item:distrib}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exponentiation]
\label{lem:exp}
Let $a$ and $b$ be codes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:exp-pos}
  $\num 1 \le \omega^{b}$.
\item\label{item:exp-mono}
  If $a \le b$ then $\omega^{a} \le \omega^{b}$.
\item\label{item:exp-double}
  $\omega^{b} \bplus \omega^{b} \le \omega^{b} \btimes \omega$, and
  hence $\bS(\omega^{b}) \le \omega^{\bS b}$.
\item\label{item:exp-incr}
  $a \le \omega^{a}$.
\item\label{item:exp-hom}
  $\omega^{\num 1} = \omega$ and
  $\omega^{a} \btimes \omega^{b} = \omega^{a \bplus b}$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{one-≤-ω\^{}};
\texttt{ω\^{}-mono}, \texttt{ω\^{}-double}, \texttt{S-ω\^{}-step},
\texttt{≤-ω\^{}}, \texttt{ω\^{}-ι1};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MultClosure}{ω\^{}⊗}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(\labelcref{item:exp-pos}) By induction on~$b$: $\omega^{\bZ} = \num 1$;
the code $\omega^{\bS b} = \omega^{b} \btimes \omega
= \bL(n \mapsto \omega^{b} \btimes \num n)$ is above
$\omega^{b} \btimes \num 1 = \omega^{b}$ by the cocone
and~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:one-unit}); and $\omega^{\bL f}$ is
above $\omega^{f\,0}$ by the cocone.

(\labelcref{item:exp-mono}) By induction on the derivation of
$a \le b$: the least element property gives
$\num 1 \le \omega^{b}$ by~(\labelcref{item:exp-pos}), the successor
rule gives $\omega^{a} \btimes \omega \le \omega^{b} \btimes \omega$
by~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:mult-mono}), and the cocone and the
limit rule are applied again, since
$\omega^{\bL f} = \bL(n \mapsto \omega^{f\,n})$.

(\labelcref{item:exp-double}) We have
$\omega^{b} \bplus \omega^{b} = \omega^{b} \btimes \num 2$
by~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:one-unit}), which is
below $\bL(n \mapsto \omega^{b} \btimes \num n) = \omega^{b} \btimes
\omega$ by the cocone, and
$\bS(\omega^{b}) = \omega^{b} \bplus \num 1 \le
\omega^{b} \bplus \omega^{b}$
by~(\labelcref{item:exp-pos}) and~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:add-mono}),
while $\omega^{\bS b} = \omega^{b} \btimes \omega$.

(\labelcref{item:exp-incr}) By induction on~$a$: $\bZ$ is least,
$\bS a \le \bS(\omega^{a}) \le \omega^{\bS a}$ by the successor rule
and~(\labelcref{item:exp-double}), and limits are handled
by~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:L-mono}).

(\labelcref{item:exp-hom}) First,
$\omega^{\num 1} = \num 1 \btimes \omega
= \bL(n \mapsto \num 1 \btimes \num n) = \bL(n \mapsto \num n)
= \omega$ by~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:one-unit}). The exponent
law is an induction on~$b$: at $\bZ$ both sides are~$\omega^{a}$
by~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:one-unit}); at a successor
$\omega^{a} \btimes (\omega^{b} \btimes \omega)
= (\omega^{a} \btimes \omega^{b}) \btimes \omega
= \omega^{a \bplus b} \btimes \omega = \omega^{a \bplus \bS b}$
by~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:mult-assoc}); and limits are handled
pointwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The tower is strictly increasing]
\label{lem:tower}
For every~$n$ we have $\tower n < \tower{n+1}$, and hence
$\tower n < \eps$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{tower-strict};
\texttt{tower-<-ε₀}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A direct induction on~$n$ has to put the successor of the limit
$\tower n$ below the limit $\tower{n+1}$, which by the cocone rule
needs a single member of the latter above the whole of the former,
and the induction hypothesis supplies none. The obstruction is
removed by~\cref{lem:exp}(\labelcref{item:exp-double}),
$\bS(\omega^{b}) \le \omega^{\bS b}$, which reduces strictness at
$\omega^{b}$ to strictness at~$b$. At $n = 0$, since
$\omega = \omega^{\num 1}$, we get
$\bS\omega \le \omega^{\num 2} \le \omega^{\omega} = \tower 1$, by
monotonicity of exponentiation and $\num 2 \le \omega$. At $n+1$,
\[
  \bS\,\tower{n+1} = \bS(\omega^{\tower n}) \le \omega^{\bS\tower n}
  \le \omega^{\tower{n+1}} = \tower{n+2} ,
\]
by monotonicity and the induction hypothesis. Finally
$\bS\,\tower n \le \tower{n+1}$ gives $\tower n < \eps$ by the cocone
at~$n+1$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{S-ω\^{}-step};
\texttt{ω\^{}-ι1}, \texttt{ω<ω\^{}ω}.}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The tower absorbs sums and products]
\label{lem:tower-absorb}
If $m \le n$ then $\tower m \le \tower n$, and for every~$n$,
\[
  \tower n \bplus \tower n \le \tower{n+1} , \qquad
  \tower n \btimes \tower n \le \tower{n+1} .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{tower-mono-step};
\texttt{tower-mono-ℕ}, \texttt{tower-double};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MultClosure}{tower-sq}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity follows by induction from
$\tower n \le \tower{n+1}$, which holds because
$\tower n \le \bS\,\tower n \le \tower{n+1}$,
by~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:S-incr}) and~\cref{lem:tower}. For the sums, at $n = 0$, with
$\omega = \omega^{\num 1}$,
\[
  \omega \bplus \omega \le \omega^{\num 1} \btimes \omega
  = \omega^{\num 2} \le \omega^{\omega}
\]
by~\cref{lem:exp}(\labelcref{item:exp-double},\labelcref{item:exp-mono}),
and at $n+1$,
\[
  \omega^{\tower n} \bplus \omega^{\tower n}
  \le \omega^{\bS\tower n} \le \omega^{\tower{n+1}}
\]
by the same two parts of~\cref{lem:exp} and~\cref{lem:tower}. For the
products we use the exponent law
of~\cref{lem:exp}(\labelcref{item:exp-hom}): at $n = 0$,
$\omega \btimes \omega = \omega^{\num 1 \bplus \num 1}
= \omega^{\num 2} \le \omega^{\omega}$, and at $n+1$,
$\omega^{\tower n} \btimes \omega^{\tower n}
= \omega^{\tower n \bplus \tower n} \le \omega^{\tower{n+1}}$ by the
case for sums and monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Closure of $\eps$]
\label{prop:closure}
If $a < \eps$ then $\bS a < \eps$ and $\omega^{a} < \eps$, and if
$b < \eps$ as well then $a \bplus b < \eps$ and $a \btimes b < \eps$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{⊕-<-ε₀}; \texttt{S-<-ε₀},
\texttt{ω\^{}-<-ε₀}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MultClosure}{⊗-<-ε₀}.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A derivation of $a < \eps$, that is of
$\bS a \le \bL(n \mapsto \tower n)$, ends with the cocone, since the
least element property and the limit rule have $\bZ$ and a limit on the
left and the successor rule has a successor on the right. So
$\bS a \le \tower j$ for some~$j$, and hence $a \le \tower j$
by~\cref{lem:order}(\labelcref{item:S-incr}). Conversely, if
$x \le \tower m$ then $x < \eps$, since
$\bS x \le \bS\,\tower m \le \tower{m+1}$ by~\cref{lem:tower} and the
cocone applies.

Now let $\bS a \le \tower j$, so that $\bS a < \eps$ by the converse.
For the other claims let also $b \le \tower k$, and let $N$ be the
larger of $j$ and~$k$, so that $a$ and~$b$ are below $\tower N$
by~\cref{lem:tower-absorb}. Then
\[
  \omega^{a} \le \omega^{\tower N} = \tower{N+1} , \qquad
  a \bplus b \le \tower N \bplus \tower N \le \tower{N+1} , \qquad
  a \btimes b \le \tower N \btimes \tower N \le \tower{N+1} ,
\]
by monotonicity of the operations, \cref{lem:add,lem:mult,lem:exp},
and by~\cref{lem:tower-absorb}, and the converse puts each of these
codes below~$\eps$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{tower-mono-ℕ};
\texttt{tower-double}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MultClosure}{tower-sq}.}
\end{proof}

The graded maps of~\cref{sec:graded} are bounded by sums of several
pieces, and the following laws absorb such sums into a single product
with a power of~$\omega$. We say that a code $x$ is below a finite
multiple of a code~$M$ when $x \le M \btimes \num n$ for some~$n$.

\begin{lemma}[Absorption]
\label{lem:absorb}
Let $a$, $b$, $c$, $e$, $s$, $x$, $y$ and $M$ be codes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:exp-law}
  $\omega^{a \bplus b} = \omega^{a} \btimes \omega^{b}$, and
  $\num 1 \le \omega^{a}$.
\item\label{item:mult-incr}
  If $\num 1 \le y$ then $x \le x \btimes y$ and $x \le y \btimes x$.
\item\label{item:double}
  $x \bplus x \le x \btimes \omega$, and
  $\num 1 \bplus x \le \bS x$.
\item\label{item:multiple}
  Every code below~$M$ is below a finite multiple of~$M$, and so is the
  sum of two codes below finite multiples of~$M$. If $x$ is below a
  finite multiple of~$M$, then
  \[
    x \btimes \omega^{e} \le M \btimes \omega^{\bS e} , \qquad
    x \le M \btimes \omega , \qquad
    x \btimes \omega \le M \btimes \omega .
  \]
\item\label{item:iterate}
  If $\num 1 \le s$, then for every~$k$ the $k$-th iterate from~$a$ of
  the map $b \mapsto (b \bplus c) \btimes \omega^{s \btimes \omega}$
  is below $(a \bplus c) \btimes
  \omega^{(s \btimes \omega) \btimes \omega}$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MultClosure}{ω\^{}⊗}; \texttt{S⊕≤S};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Epsilon0}{one-≤-ω\^{}}, \texttt{x-≤-x⊗},
\texttt{y-≤-⊗}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MoveTuple}{dup-ω};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Absorb}{\_⋖\_}, \texttt{⋖-base}, \texttt{⋖-⊕},
\texttt{ι⊗ω\^{}}, \texttt{⋖-⊗}, \texttt{⋖-⊗ω}, \texttt{⋖-ω};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MultAffine}{dbl-ω\^{}}, \texttt{crux},
\texttt{orbit-inv}, \texttt{pw-ω\^{}};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Move}{padMove}, \texttt{move-orbit}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part~(\labelcref{item:exp-law}) is
\cref{lem:exp}(\labelcref{item:exp-hom},\labelcref{item:exp-pos}). For
(\labelcref{item:mult-incr}), $x = x \btimes \num 1 = \num 1 \btimes
x$ by~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:one-unit}), and multiplication
is monotone in each argument. For
(\labelcref{item:double}), $x \bplus x = x \btimes \num 2 \le
x \btimes \omega$, and $\num 1 \bplus x \le \bS x$ is an induction
on~$x$.

For (\labelcref{item:multiple}), a code below~$M$ is below
$M \btimes \num 1$, and sums are handled by left distributivity,
$M \btimes \num k \bplus M \btimes \num l = M \btimes \num{k+l}$. The
three inequalities follow from $x \le M \btimes \num n$ by
associativity of multiplication and three facts about a
numeral~$\num n$: $\num n \le \omega$; $\num n \btimes \omega \le
\omega$, because each $\num n \btimes \num j$ is a numeral; and
$\num n \btimes \omega^{e} \le \omega^{\bS e}$, by induction on~$e$.
In this last induction, at $e = \bZ$ the inequality reads
$\num n \btimes \num 1 = \num n \le \omega = \omega^{\num 1}$,
by~\cref{lem:mult}(\labelcref{item:one-unit})
and~\cref{lem:exp}(\labelcref{item:exp-hom}). At a successor,
\[
  \num n \btimes \omega^{\bS e}
  = (\num n \btimes \omega^{e}) \btimes \omega
  \le \omega^{\bS e} \btimes \omega = \omega^{\bS\bS e} ,
\]
by associativity of multiplication, the induction hypothesis and
monotonicity of multiplication on the left. At a limit
$e = \bL f$, the left-hand side is the limit
\[
  \num n \btimes \omega^{\bL f}
  = \bL\big(k \mapsto \num n \btimes \omega^{f\,k}\big) ,
\]
and each of its members is below the right-hand side,
\[
  \num n \btimes \omega^{f\,k} \le \omega^{\bS(f\,k)}
  = \omega^{f\,k} \btimes \omega
  \le \omega^{\bL f} \btimes \omega = \omega^{\bS\bL f} ,
\]
by the induction hypothesis at $f\,k$, by the cocone
$\omega^{f\,k} \le \bL(j \mapsto \omega^{f\,j}) = \omega^{\bL f}$ and
by monotonicity of multiplication on the left, so that the limit rule
applies.

For (\labelcref{item:iterate}), write $N = \omega^{s \btimes \omega}$.
Doubling is absorbed by~$N$, that is $\num 2 \btimes N \le N$, because
$N$ is the limit of the codes $\omega^{s \btimes \num k}$ and
\[
  \num 2 \btimes \omega^{s \btimes \num k}
  \le \omega \btimes \omega^{s \btimes \num k}
  = \omega^{\num 1 \bplus s \btimes \num k}
  \le \omega^{s \btimes \num{k+1}}
\]
by $\num 2 \le \omega$ and monotonicity, by (\labelcref{item:exp-law}),
by (\labelcref{item:double}) and by $\num 1 \le s$. Hence $c \le Y$
implies
$(Y \bplus c) \btimes N \le (Y \btimes \num 2) \btimes N \le
Y \btimes N$. Writing $N^{0} = \num 1$ and
$N^{k+1} = N^{k} \btimes N$, the $k$-th iterate is then below
$(a \bplus c) \btimes N^{k}$, by induction on~$k$, since at each step
$c$ is below $(a \bplus c) \btimes N^{k}$. Finally
$N^{k} \le \omega^{(s \btimes \omega) \btimes \num k} \le
\omega^{(s \btimes \omega) \btimes \omega}$ by
(\labelcref{item:exp-law}) and monotonicity.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The height of a dialogue tree]
\label{def:height}
The height of a dialogue tree is the Brouwer code
\[
  \height(\eta\,n) = \bZ , \qquad
  \height(\beta\,\varphi\,i) = \bL(n \mapsto \bS\,\height(\varphi\,n)) ,
\]
that is, $0$ at a leaf and the supremum of the heights of the subtrees
raised by one at a query node. The query index is ignored, the height
depending on the branching structure alone.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Constructive}{height}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Relabelling, grafting and the generic oracle]
\label{lem:height-three}
Let $f : \N\to\N$, let $g : \N \to \Dial\N$, let $b : \Brouwer$ and let
$d : \Dial\N$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:relabel}
  $\height(\Dial f\,d) = \height(d)$.
\item\label{item:graft}
  If $\height(g\,k) \le b$ for every~$k$, then
  $\height(\kext g\,d) \le b \bplus \height(d)$.
\item\label{item:generic}
  $\height(\mathrm{generic}\,d) \le \bS\,\height(d)$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Constructive}{height-relabel};
\texttt{height-kleisli-extension}, \texttt{height-generic}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All three are inductions on~$d$. In
(\labelcref{item:relabel}) the height ignores the values at the
leaves, and this is the only one of the three that uses function
extensionality, to pass from a pointwise identification of two
families to an identification of their limits.
In (\labelcref{item:graft}) the summand~$b$ is on the left because the
structure of~$d$ lies above the grafted trees, and the successor step
is unconditional, which is what makes the proof constructive. Part
(\labelcref{item:generic}) amounts to grafting a single query, since
$\mathrm{generic} = \kext{(\beta\,\eta)}$, and is proved by an
induction of its own rather than as an instance
of~(\labelcref{item:graft}).
\end{proof}

\section{Majorants and the reduction to ordinal arithmetic}
\label{sec:majorant}

We assign to each term a hereditary majorant, extend the assignment to
the recursor at every type by equipping the majorants with a pointwise
supremum and a pointwise translation, and reduce the theorem to the
statement that the majorant of a closed term of ground type is
below~$\eps$.

\begin{definition}[Majorants]
\label{def:majorant}
The type of majorants of a system T type is defined by recursion on
the type,
\[
  \Maj(\gr) = \Brouwer , \qquad
  \Maj(\sigma\arr\tau) = \Maj(\sigma) \to \Maj(\tau) ,
\]
and so is the relation of being majorized:
\begin{align*}
  x \maj_{\gr} a &\quad\text{means}\quad \height(x) \le a , \\
  F \maj_{\sigma\arr\tau} \varphi &\quad\text{means that}\quad
    x \maj_{\sigma} a \ \text{implies}\ F\,x \maj_{\tau} \varphi\,a ,
\end{align*}
the latter for all $x$ and~$a$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Majorant}{Maj}; \texttt{R}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Majorants of the combinators]
\label{def:majorant-combinators}
The constant $\Zeroc$ has majorant~$\bZ$, the successor has the
identity, since relabelling preserves the height, the oracle has the
successor $a \mapsto \bS a$, and $\Kc$ and~$\Sc$ have the projection
$(a,b)\mapsto a$ and the substitution
$(\varphi,\gamma,a) \mapsto \varphi\,a\,(\gamma\,a)$. Each combinator
is majorized by its majorant.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Majorant}{μ-Zero}; \texttt{μ-Succ},
\texttt{μ-Ω}, \texttt{μ-K}, \texttt{μ-S}, \texttt{R-Zero},
\texttt{R-Succ}, \texttt{R-Ω}, \texttt{R-K}, \texttt{R-S}.}
\end{definition}

The recursor is the remaining constant. At ground type its majorant is
the supremum of the iterates of a family of codes, and at a type
$\sigma\arr\tau$ a majorant was missing. We supply one at every type.

\begin{definition}[Supremum and translation]
\label{def:maj-sup}
The pointwise supremum and the pointwise right translation on
majorants are defined by recursion on the type, acting under the
argument at an arrow:
\begin{align*}
  \textstyle\sup_{\gr} h &= \bL h , &
  a \bplus_{\gr} \nu &= a \bplus \nu , \\
  \textstyle(\sup_{\sigma\arr\tau} h)\,a
    &= \textstyle\sup_{\tau} (k \mapsto h\,k\,a) , &
  (\varphi \bplus_{\sigma\arr\tau} \nu)\,a
    &= (\varphi\,a) \bplus_{\tau} \nu .
\end{align*}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.MajorantAll}{Maj-sup};
\texttt{Maj-⊕},
\texttt{μ-Kleisli}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The hereditary Kleisli bound]
\label{lem:kleisli-bound}
Let $g : \N \to \dsem\sigma$ and $h : \N \to \Maj(\sigma)$ satisfy
$g\,k \maj_{\sigma} h\,k$ for every~$k$, and let $d$ be a dialogue tree
with $\height(d) \le \nu$. Then
\[
  \kext g\,d \ \maj_{\sigma}\
  \big(\textstyle\sup_{\sigma} h\big) \bplus_{\sigma} \nu .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.MajorantAll}{R-Kleisli}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on~$\sigma$. At the ground type the grafting lemma
applies with the bound $\bL h$, which the upper bound property of the
limit supplies, and monotonicity of addition in its right argument
then passes from $\height(d)$ to~$\nu$; see
\cref{lem:height-three}(\labelcref{item:graft}). The arrow case holds
by definition on both
sides, the Kleisli extension, the supremum and the translation all
passing under the argument in the same way.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The majorant of a term]
\label{def:majorant-term}
The majorant of the recursor at type~$\sigma$ is
\[
  \Mj_{\Iterc}\,\varphi\,a\,\nu
  = \big(\textstyle\sup_{\sigma}(k \mapsto \iterop\,\varphi\,a\,k)\big)
     \bplus_{\sigma} \nu ,
\]
and the majorant $\Mj(t)$ of a term of~$\TmO$ is defined by the
constants above and $\Mj(t\cdot u) = \Mj(t)\,\Mj(u)$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.MajorantAll}{μ-Iterᵀ}; \texttt{μᵀ},
\texttt{R-Iterᵀ}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The fundamental theorem for majorants]
\label{thm:fundamental-majorant}
Every closed term $t$ of~$\TmO\sigma$ satisfies
$\dsem t \maj_{\sigma} \Mj(t)$. In particular, for a closed term
$t : (\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ of system T,
\[
  \height(\dtree(t)) \le \Mj(\overline t \cdot \Omega) .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.MajorantAll}{fundamentalᵀ};
\texttt{dialogue-tree-height-≤-μᵀ}.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the term. Every case but the recursor is the majorant
of the corresponding combinator, and the recursor is
\cref{lem:kleisli-bound} applied to the iterates, which are majorized
by induction on the iteration count.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The reduction]
\label{thm:reduction}
Let $t : (\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ be a closed term of system T. If
$\Mj(\overline t \cdot \Omega) < \eps$ then
$\height(\dtree(t)) < \eps$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.MajorantAll}{dialogue-height-<-ε₀}.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:fundamental-majorant} bounds the height by the majorant, and
the successor is monotone, so
\[
  \bS\,\height(\dtree(t)) \ \le\ \bS\,\Mj(\overline t \cdot \Omega)
  \ \le\ \eps ,
\]
the second inequality being the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis concerns Brouwer codes alone, $\Mj(t)$ being a
hereditary functional over codes built from addition, suprema and
suprema of iterates. The rest of the paper proves that hypothesis, in
the form $\Mj(t) < \eps$ for every closed term $t$ of~$\TmO\gr$.

\section{Graded maps}
\label{sec:graded}

We define the data and their grading, the scalar maps between them
whose exponents are again maps, and the rectangular maps between data
of any two widths with the operations on them, and we prove the
calculus of these maps that the induction on terms uses.

\subsection{Data and their grading}
\label{sec:data}

A \emph{datum of width~$n$} is an $n$-tuple of Brouwer codes, written
$\Brouwer^{n}$. Its entry at position~$j$ is its entry \emph{of
order~$j$}, where order~$0$ is a translation, order~$1$ an exponent,
order~$2$ the exponent of an exponent, and so on. We write $\tsum t$
for the sum of the entries of~$t$, we write $t \tle u$ for the
componentwise order on data of equal width, and we say that a datum is
\emph{small} when each of its entries is below~$\eps$. A datum $t$ of
width~$k$ sits inside a datum $u$ of width~$n$, written $t \tsub u$,
when $k \le n$ and each entry of~$t$ is below the entry of~$u$ of the
same order.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.MoveTuple}{Tup}; \texttt{tsum},
\texttt{\_⊑\_}, \texttt{All<}, \texttt{tsum-mono},
\texttt{tsum-<-ε₀}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectAlgebra}{\_⊴\_}.}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:grading-rule}
One rule governs every construction below: an entry of the output is
raised only by entries of the input of its own order or higher. The
rule is what the iterates of $x \mapsto \omega^{x}$ break, since each
iterate raises the exponent by the value below it, and it is what
keeps the whole construction below~$\eps$, by
\cref{prop:closure}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scalar maps and cascades}
\label{sec:cascades}

At the ground type the maps needed are of the form
$a \mapsto (a \bplus t) \btimes \omega^{s}$, with a fixed
exponent~$s$. The combinator~$\Sc$ asks for
more, since the datum of $f\,a\,(g\,a)$ carries an exponent that varies
with~$a$, so we let the exponent be a map of the same kind one order
up.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Move}{Move}; \texttt{img},
\texttt{orbit}, \texttt{move-orbit}, \texttt{padMove}.}

\begin{definition}[Cascades]
\label{def:cascade}
A \emph{cascade} of width~$k$ is a tuple of $k+1$ codes, one per
order of the datum it reads and a last one standing alone, and its
action on a datum of width~$k$ is defined by
\[
  \act{(c,E)}{(a,t)} = \big((a \bplus \tsum t) \bplus c\big)
    \btimes \omega^{\act{E}{t}} , \qquad
  \act{c}{\star} = c ,
\]
so that it reads the whole datum in its base and only the tail in its
exponent, the exponent being again a cascade. A cascade is
\emph{small} when each of its codes is below~$\eps$. A cascade of
width~$k$ and a tuple of width $k+1$ hold the same codes, and we read
each as the other without further notice.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Cascade}{Sc}; \texttt{valSc},
\texttt{ScAll<}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.CascadeLevel}{tupToSc},
\texttt{scToTup}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:last-grade}
The recursion ends at a free constant rather than at a translation,
and the choice is forced. A cascade whose exponent at the last grade
were read off a grade below it has no grade below to read, so that
exponent would be~$\bZ$; since $\omega^{\bZ} = \bS\,\bZ$ and
$x \btimes \bS\,\bZ = x$, the last grade would act by a translation,
and a translation cannot absorb a doubling: with $x$ the entry and $t$
the translation, $x \bplus x \le x \bplus t$ fails at
$x = t \bplus 1$, whatever $t$ is, since $x$ is chosen after it. With
a constant that the cascade carries itself, a doubling is paid at
every grade by one successor on the grade above.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Cascade}{valSc-join};
\texttt{valSc-suc}.}
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:cascade-basic}
A small cascade takes a small datum to a code below~$\eps$. The value
of a cascade is monotone in the datum and in the codes of the
cascade, and the value of a cascade of positive width at a datum is
above the first entry of the datum and above the first code of the
cascade.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Cascade}{valSc-<-ε₀};
\texttt{valSc-mono}, \texttt{valSc-increasing}, \texttt{valSc-const};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.CascadeLevel}{valSc-⊑}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the width. The first part follows
from~\cref{prop:closure} and the second from monotonicity of addition,
multiplication and exponentiation. For the third, the base
$(a \bplus \tsum t) \bplus c$ is above $a$ and above~$c$, and the
multiplier $\omega^{\act E t}$ is at least~$\num 1$, so
\cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:exp-law},\labelcref{item:mult-incr})
applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Operations on cascades]
\label{def:cascade-ops}
For cascades $E$, $E_1$ and $E_2$ of width~$k$ we define three
cascades of width~$k$ by recursion on~$k$. The successor $E^{+}$
takes the successor of every code, the join $E_1 \vee E_2$ adds the
codes of the first grade and joins the rest with one successor, and
the bump $E^{\times}$ multiplies a constant by~$\omega$ and takes the
successor of an exponent:
\begin{align*}
  c^{+} &= \bS c , &
  (c, E)^{+} &= (\bS c, E^{+}) , \\
  c_1 \vee c_2 &= c_1 \bplus c_2 , &
  (c_1, E_1) \vee (c_2, E_2)
    &= (c_1 \bplus c_2, (E_1 \vee E_2)^{+}) , \\
  c^{\times} &= c \btimes \omega , &
  (c, E)^{\times} &= (c, E^{+}) .
\end{align*}
The widening of~$E$ is the cascade $(\num 1, E)$ of width $k+1$, and
the zero cascade~$0$ is the cascade whose codes are all~$\bZ$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Cascade}{sucSc}; \texttt{joinSc},
\texttt{bumpSc}, \texttt{upSc}, \texttt{zeroSc}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The operations on cascades]
\label{lem:cascade-ops}
Let $E$, $E_1$ and $E_2$ be cascades of width~$k$, let $t$ be a datum
of width~$k$, and let $a$ and $v$ be codes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:suc}
  $\bS(\act E t) \le \act{E^{+}}{t}$.
\item\label{item:join}
  $\act{E_1}{t} \bplus \act{E_2}{t} \le \act{(E_1 \vee E_2)}{t}$.
\item\label{item:bump}
  $\act E t \btimes \omega \le \act{E^{\times}}{t}$.
\item\label{item:up}
  $\act E t \le \act{(\num 1, E)}{(a,t)}$.
\item\label{item:fold}
  For the cascade $(c, E)$ of width $k+1$ and the datum $(a,t)$,
  \[
    \act{(c,E)}{(a,t)} \bplus v \le \act{(c \bplus v, E^{+})}{(a,t)} .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
The successor, the join, the bump and the widening take small cascades
to small cascades, and the zero cascade is small.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Cascade}{valSc-suc}; \texttt{valSc-join},
\texttt{valSc-bump}, \texttt{valSc-up}, \texttt{sucSc-All<},
\texttt{joinSc-All<}, \texttt{bumpSc-All<}, \texttt{upSc-All<},
\texttt{zeroSc-All<}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectAdd}{valSc-fold}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part~(\labelcref{item:up}) holds because
$\act E t \le \omega^{\act E t} \le ((a \bplus \tsum t) \bplus \num 1)
\btimes \omega^{\act E t}$, by \cref{lem:exp}(\labelcref{item:exp-incr})
and \cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:mult-incr}). The other parts are
inductions on~$k$, and at width~$0$ the first three are equalities.
At width $k+1$, write the datum as $(a,t)$, put $s = a \bplus \tsum t$,
and write $X$, $X_1$ and $X_2$ for the values at~$t$ of the exponents
of $E = (c, E')$, $E_1 = (c_1, E_1')$ and $E_2 = (c_2, E_2')$.

For~(\labelcref{item:suc}), let $V = \bS(s \bplus c) \btimes \omega^{X}$.
Then $(s \bplus c) \btimes \omega^{X} \le V$ and $\num 1 \le V$, so
\[
  \bS\big((s \bplus c) \btimes \omega^{X}\big)
  \le \bS V \le V \bplus V \le V \btimes \omega
  = \bS(s \bplus c) \btimes \omega^{\bS X}
\]
by~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:add-mono}), since $\bS V = V \bplus
\num 1$, and
\cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:double},\labelcref{item:exp-law}),
and $\bS X$ is below the value of $E'^{+}$
at~$t$ by induction, while $\bS(s \bplus c) = s \bplus \bS c$ is the
base of~$E^{+}$.

For~(\labelcref{item:join}), let
$W = (s \bplus (c_1 \bplus c_2)) \btimes \omega^{X_1 \bplus X_2}$. Both
values are below~$W$, so their sum is below
$W \bplus W \le W \btimes \omega
 = (s \bplus (c_1 \bplus c_2)) \btimes \omega^{\bS(X_1 \bplus X_2)}$,
and $\bS(X_1 \bplus X_2)$ is below the value of
$(E_1' \vee E_2')^{+}$ at~$t$ by induction
and~(\labelcref{item:suc}).

For~(\labelcref{item:bump}),
$\big((s \bplus c) \btimes \omega^{X}\big) \btimes \omega
 = (s \bplus c) \btimes \omega^{\bS X}$, and (\labelcref{item:suc})
applies to the exponent.

For~(\labelcref{item:fold}), where now $X = \act E t$, the code
$Q = (s \bplus (c \bplus v)) \btimes \omega^{X}$ is above the value
of $(c, E)$ at $(a,t)$ and above~$v$, so the sum is below
$Q \bplus Q \le Q \btimes \omega
 = (s \bplus (c \bplus v)) \btimes \omega^{\bS X}$, and
(\labelcref{item:suc}) applies to the exponent. Smallness is preserved
by~\cref{prop:closure}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rectangular maps}
\label{sec:rect}

\begin{definition}[Graded maps]
\label{def:rect}
A \emph{graded map} from width~$p$ to width~$q$ is given, for each
output order~$j$, by a cascade reading the input orders~$j$ and above.
Its action is
\[
  \act{(E,D)}{(a,t)} = \big(\act{E}{(a,t)} , \act{D}{t}\big) ,
\]
the first component being a cascade on the whole input and the rest a
graded map applied to the tail. The datum of such a map is held as the
triangular array it is, one cascade per output order, each shorter
than the last, and a bare constant once the input orders are
exhausted, so that a graded map from width~$0$ is a tuple of
constants. For a graded map $F = (E, F')$ from a positive width we
call $E$ its cascade of order~$0$ and $F'$ its tail. A graded map is
\emph{small} when its codes are below~$\eps$. For graded maps $F$
and~$G$ of the same widths we write $F \tle G$ when each code of~$F$
is below the corresponding code of~$G$, and then
$\act F x \tle \act G x$ for every datum~$x$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Rect}{RD}; \texttt{rect},
\texttt{RDAll<},
\texttt{rect-All<}, \texttt{rect-increasing}, \texttt{\_⊑RD\_},
\texttt{rect-⊑RD}.}
\end{definition}

For the datum of a map to be a datum in its turn it has to be a tuple
as well. Writing $\W(p,q) = \max(p+1,q)$, a tuple
$d = (d_0, \ldots, d_{\W(p,q)-1})$ is read as a graded map $\rd d$
from width~$p$ to width~$q$, as follows. For an output order
$j \le p$ the cascade is $(d_j, \ldots, d_p)$, the entries of~$d$ of
orders $j$ to~$p$, which is a cascade of width $p-j$, and for
$p \le j < q$ it is the constant~$d_j$, the two clauses agreeing at
$j = p$. Equivalently, for positive $p$ and~$q$ the cascade of
order~$0$ of $\rd d$ is read off the first $p+1$ entries of~$d$ and
the tail of $\rd d$ is $\rd{d'}$ for the tail $d'$ of~$d$, since
$\W(p,q) = 1 + \W(p-1,q-1)$. The entries of orders up to~$p$ are
shared between output orders, which is what makes the datum of a map
fit into a width one more than that of the input, and the entries
above order~$p$ are read as constants only, which is what makes it
fit into the width of the output. The reading is monotone:
$d \tle d'$ implies $\rd d \tle \rd{d'}$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Rect}{WD}; \texttt{rdOf}, \texttt{pre},
\texttt{rdOf-All<}, \texttt{rdOf-mono}.}

\begin{definition}[Operations on graded maps]
\label{def:rect-ops}
Let $F$ and $G$ be graded maps from width~$p$ to width~$q$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The zero map $\mathrm{zero}$ has every cascade the zero
  cascade.
\item The join $F \vee G$ joins the cascades of $F$ and~$G$ order by
  order, constants being added.
\item The sum $\tsum F$ is the cascade of width~$p$ defined by
  $\tsum (E, F') = E \vee (\num 1, \tsum F')$ for $p$ and $q$
  positive, as the sum of the constants for $p = 0$, and as the zero
  cascade for $q = 0$.
\item The cut, to width~$q$, of a map to width $\max(q,q')$ keeps its
  first~$q$ output orders, and the widening, to width $\max(q,q')$, of
  a map to width~$q$ adds zero cascades at the new output orders.
\item The flattening of~$F$ is the tuple $\flat F$ of width
  $\W(p,q)$ defined as follows. For $p$ and $q$ positive and
  $F = ((c, E'), F')$, it is $c$ followed by $\flat F'$ with the $p$
  codes of~$E'$ added slotwise into its first $p$ entries. For $p = 0$
  it is the tuple of constants, and for $q = 0$ the zero tuple of
  width $p+1$.
\item For $p$ positive and $F = (E, F')$, the translation of~$F$ is
  the map $F^{\dagger} = (E \vee 0, F'^{+})$, where $F'^{+}$ takes
  the successor of every cascade and every constant of~$F'$.
\end{enumerate}
For a datum $t = (t_0, t')$ and a code~$v$ we write
$t^{+v} = (t_0 \bplus v, t'^{+})$, where $t'^{+}$ takes the successor
of every entry.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectAlgebra}{zeroRD}; \texttt{joinRD},
\texttt{padRD}, \texttt{flatRD}, \texttt{addRDc};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectSum}{sumRD};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectApply}{preRD};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectAdd}{sucRD}, \texttt{addν}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The operations on graded maps]
\label{lem:rect-ops}
Let $F$ and $G$ be graded maps from width~$p$ to width~$q$, and let
$x$ be a datum of width~$p$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:rjoin}
  $F \tle F \vee G$ and $G \tle F \vee G$.
\item\label{item:rsum}
  $\tsum(\act F x) \le \act{(\tsum F)}{x}$.
\item\label{item:rcut}
  The cut of the widening of a map is the map itself, and the action
  of the cut of a map is the corresponding prefix of the action of the
  map.
\item\label{item:rflat}
  $F \tle \rd{\flat F}$.
\item\label{item:rtrans}
  If $p$ is positive and $v$ is below the first entry of~$x$, then
  $(\act F x)^{+v} \tle \act{F^{\dagger}}{x}$.
\item\label{item:rread}
  For a tuple $d$ of width $\W(p,q)$ and positive~$q$,
  $(\act{\rd d}{x})^{+v} \tle \act{\rd{d^{+v}}}{x}$.
\end{enumerate}
Each of the operations takes small maps to small maps, and the zero
map is small.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectAlgebra}{joinRD-left};
\texttt{joinRD-right}, \texttt{rect-padRD}, \texttt{flatRD-⊒},
\texttt{rect-addνc}, \texttt{zeroRD-All<}, \texttt{joinRD-All<},
\texttt{padRD-All<}, \texttt{flatRD-All<}, \texttt{addRDc-All<};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectSum}{tsum-rect}, \texttt{sumRD-All<};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectApply}{rect-preRD}, \texttt{preRD-All<};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectAdd}{rect-sucRD}, \texttt{rect-addν}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part~(\labelcref{item:rjoin}) holds code by code, since $c_1$ and
$c_2$ are below $c_1 \bplus c_2$ and a code is below its successor.
Part~(\labelcref{item:rsum}) is an induction on the widths: with
$x = (a,t)$,
$\tsum(\act F x) = \act E x \bplus \tsum(\act{F'}{t})$, and
$\tsum(\act{F'}{t}) \le \act{(\tsum F')}{t} \le
\act{(\num 1, \tsum F')}{x}$ by induction and
\cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:up}), so
\cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:join}) applies.
Part~(\labelcref{item:rcut}) is immediate from the definitions. For
(\labelcref{item:rflat}), the cascade of order~$0$ of $\rd{\flat F}$
is read off the first $p+1$ entries of $\flat F$, which are $c$
followed by codes above those of~$E'$, and the tail of $\rd{\flat F}$
is read off a tuple above $\flat F'$, so induction and monotonicity of
the reading apply. For (\labelcref{item:rtrans}), at order~$0$ we have
$\act E x \bplus v \le \act E x \bplus \act 0 x \le
\act{(E \vee 0)}{x}$, since $v$ is below the first entry of~$x$,
which is below $\act 0 x$ by~\cref{lem:cascade-basic}, and at the
orders above \cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:suc}) applies to
each cascade. For (\labelcref{item:rread}), the cascade of order~$0$
of $\rd{d^{+v}}$ is $(d_0 \bplus v, E^{+})$ when that of $\rd d$ is
$(d_0, E)$, so \cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:fold}) applies
at order~$0$ for positive~$p$, the case $p = 0$ being an equality, and
the cascades of the orders above are those of $\rd d$ with a successor
on every code, so that \cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:suc})
applies. Smallness is preserved by~\cref{prop:closure}
and~\cref{lem:cascade-ops}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The calculus}
\label{sec:calculus}

\begin{lemma}[The calculus]
\label{lem:calculus}
Graded maps support application, abstraction, projection and
iteration. For a datum $y$ of width~$p$ and a datum $x$ of width~$n$,
write $y \sqcup x$ for their slotwise sum, the datum of width
$\max(p,n)$ whose entry of order~$i$ is $y_i \bplus x_i$ when both
entries exist and the entry that exists otherwise.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:apply}
  Application. For maps $F$ from width~$m$ to width $\W(p,q)$ and $G$
  from width~$m$ to width~$p$ there is a map $F\cdot G$ from width~$m$
  to width~$q$ with
  \[
    \act{\rd{\act F x}}{\act G x} \tle \act{(F\cdot G)}{x}
  \]
  for every datum~$x$ of width~$m$: a map applied to the result of
  another map of the same argument is a map of that argument.
\item\label{item:curry}
  Abstraction. For a map $D$ from width $\max(p,n)$ to width~$q$ there
  is a map $\Lambda D$ from width~$n$ to width $\W(p,q)$ with
  \[
    \act{D}{y \sqcup x} \tle \act{\rd{\act{(\Lambda D)}{x}}}{y}
  \]
  for all $y$ of width~$p$ and $x$ of width~$n$: a map of a combined
  argument is read back as a map of one part whose output acts on the
  other.
\item\label{item:var}
  Projection. If $t \tsub u$ then $t \tle \act{\mathrm{zero}}{u}$,
  where $\mathrm{zero}$ is the zero map, so a variable is bounded by
  its context.
\item\label{item:orbit}
  Iteration. Let $e$ be a datum of width $\W(p,p)$, let $x$ have
  width~$p$ and let $u$ be a small datum of width~$n$ with $e \tsub u$
  and $x \tsub u$. There is a map~$R$ from width~$n$ to width~$p$,
  depending only on those two widths, with
  \[
    \rd e^{\,j}(x) \tle \act{R}{u} \qquad \text{for every } j ,
  \]
  so the iterates of a square map applied to a datum inside a context
  are bounded by an action of that context, uniformly in the
  iteration count.
\end{enumerate}
In each case the resulting map is small when the given ones are.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectApply}{rect-ssub}; \texttt{sh},
\texttt{sh-All<}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectCurry}{rect-curry},
\texttt{curryD}, \texttt{curryD-All<}, \texttt{cmb};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectAlgebra}{rect-⊴}, \texttt{zeroRD},
\texttt{flatRD}; \Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectOrbitAct}{rorbit-act},
\texttt{rorbitD}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We prove projection first, then application, abstraction and
iteration, each of the last three using the ones before it.

Projection. The entry of order~$i$ of $\act{\mathrm{zero}}{u}$ is the
value of the zero cascade at the suffix of~$u$ from~$i$, which is
above~$u_i$ by~\cref{lem:cascade-basic}, and $u_i$ is above~$t_i$.

Application. We first treat a single output order. For maps $F$ from
width~$m$ to width $p+1$ and $G$ from width~$m$ to width~$p$ we
construct a cascade $A(F,G)$ of width~$m$ such that, for every
datum~$x$ of width~$m$, the tuple $\act F x$, read as a cascade of
width~$p$, takes $\act G x$ to a code below $\act{A(F,G)}{x}$. If
$p = 0$, the cascade read off $\act F x$ is its single entry, which is
the value at~$x$ of the cascade of order~$0$ of~$F$, and $A(F,G)$ is
that cascade. If $m = 0$, the maps are constant, and $A(F,G)$ is the
code obtained. If $m$ and~$p$ are positive, write $x = (a,t)$,
$F = (E_F, F')$ and $G = (E_G, G')$, so that
$\act F x = (\act{E_F}{x}, \act{F'}{t})$ and
$\act G x = (\act{E_G}{x}, \act{G'}{t})$. The code to be bounded is
\[
  \big((\act{E_G}{x} \bplus \tsum(\act{G'}{t})) \bplus
  \act{E_F}{x}\big) \btimes \omega^{X} ,
\]
where $X$ is the value at $\act{G'}{t}$ of the tuple $\act{F'}{t}$
read as a cascade, so that $X \le \act{A(F',G')}{t}$ by induction. The
sum $\tsum(\act{G'}{t})$ is below $\tsum(\act G x)$, hence below
$\act{(\tsum G)}{x}$ by~\cref{lem:rect-ops}, and
\cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:join}) puts the base below
$\act J x$ for the cascade $J = (E_G \vee \tsum G) \vee E_F$. Writing
$J = (c_J, E_J)$ and $s = a \bplus \tsum t$, the code is therefore
below
\[
  \act J x \btimes \omega^{\act{A(F',G')}{t}}
  = (s \bplus c_J) \btimes
    \omega^{\act{E_J}{t} \bplus \act{A(F',G')}{t}}
  \le (s \bplus c_J) \btimes
    \omega^{\act{(E_J \vee A(F',G'))}{t}}
\]
by \cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:exp-law}) and
\cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:join}), and we put
$A(F,G) = (c_J, E_J \vee A(F',G'))$.

Now let $F$ go from width~$m$ to width $\W(p,q)$ and $G$ from
width~$m$ to width~$p$. If $q = 0$ there is nothing to prove. If
$p = 0$, the map $\rd{\act F x}$ consists of the constants
$\act F x$, and $F \cdot G = F$. Otherwise the output of order~$0$
of $\rd{\act F x}$ at $\act G x$ is the value at $\act G x$ of the
first $p+1$ entries of $\act F x$, read as a cascade. Those entries
are $\act{F_0}{x}$ for the cut $F_0$ of~$F$ to width $p+1$,
by~\cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rcut}), so this output is
below $\act{A(F_0,G)}{x}$. The outputs of the orders above are
$\rd{\act{F'}{t}}$ at $\act{G'}{t}$, where $F'$ and~$G'$ are the
tails of $F$ and~$G$, of widths $\W(p-1,q-1)$ and $p-1$, and $t$ is
the tail of~$x$, or the empty datum when $m = 0$. We put
$F \cdot G = (A(F_0,G), F' \cdot G')$ and conclude by induction.

Abstraction. Again we first treat a single output order. For a
cascade~$E$ of width $\max(p,n)$ we construct a map $\Lambda_0 E$ from
width~$n$ to width $p+1$ such that, for all $y$ of width~$p$ and $x$
of width~$n$, the tuple $\act{(\Lambda_0 E)}{x}$, read as a cascade of
width~$p$, takes~$y$ to a code above
$\bS(\act{E}{y \sqcup x}) \btimes \omega$. If $p = 0$, then
$y \sqcup x = x$, and $\Lambda_0 E$ is the map with the single
cascade $(E^{+})^{\times}$; by
\cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:suc},\labelcref{item:bump}),
$\bS(\act E x) \btimes \omega \le \act{E^{+}}{x} \btimes \omega \le
\act{(E^{+})^{\times}}{x}$. If $n = 0$, then $y \sqcup x = y$, and
$\Lambda_0 E$ is the constant map with output the tuple
$(E^{+})^{\times}$, by the same inequality. If $p$ and~$n$ are
positive, write $E = (c, E')$, $y = (y_0, y')$ and $x = (x_0, x')$,
and let $\Lambda_0 E = ((\bS c, 0), \Lambda_0 E')$, whose cascade of
order~$0$ is $(\bS c, 0)$. Put
\begin{align*}
  A_0 &= \act{(\bS c, 0)}{x} , &
  b_R &= (y_0 \bplus \tsum y') \bplus A_0 , \\
  & &
  b_L &= ((y_0 \bplus x_0) \bplus \tsum(y' \sqcup x')) \bplus c ,
\end{align*}
let $X_L = \act{E'}{y' \sqcup x'}$, and let $X_R$ be the value at~$y'$
of the tuple $\act{(\Lambda_0 E')}{x'}$ read as a cascade. Then
$\act{E}{y \sqcup x} = b_L \btimes \omega^{X_L}$, the code to be
bounded from below is $b_R \btimes \omega^{X_R}$, and
$\bS(X_L) \btimes \omega \le X_R$ by induction. The code~$A_0$ is
above its base $(x_0 \bplus \tsum x') \bplus \bS c$, so the codes
$x_0$, $\tsum x'$, $c$ and~$\num 1$ are below $A_0$, hence below
$b_R$, and so are $y_0$ and~$\tsum y'$. Each entry of $y' \sqcup x'$
is an entry of~$y'$, an entry of~$x'$ or the sum of one of each, so
by~\cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:multiple}) the sum
$\tsum(y' \sqcup x')$ and then $b_L$ are below a finite multiple
of~$b_R$, and
\[
  \act{E}{y \sqcup x} = b_L \btimes \omega^{X_L}
  \le b_R \btimes \omega^{\bS X_L} = Y .
\]
Since $\num 1 \le b_R \le Y$, the code $\bS(\act{E}{y \sqcup x})$ is
below a finite multiple of~$Y$, so that
$\bS(\act{E}{y \sqcup x}) \btimes \omega \le Y \btimes \omega =
b_R \btimes \omega^{\bS\bS X_L}$ by
\cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:multiple},\labelcref{item:exp-law}).
Finally $\bS\bS X_L$ is below a finite multiple of $\bS X_L$, hence
below $(\bS X_L) \btimes \omega \le X_R$.

Now let $D$ go from width $\max(p,n)$ to width~$q$. If $q = 0$,
$\Lambda D$ is the zero map. If $p = 0$, then $\Lambda D = D$, since
$y$ is empty and $\rd{\act D x}$ consists of the constants
$\act D x$. If $n = 0$, then $\Lambda D$ is the constant map with
output $\flat D$, and \cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rflat})
applies. If $p$, $n$ and~$q$ are positive, write $D = (E, D')$, with
$E$ of width $\max(p,n)$ and $D'$ from width $\max(p-1,n-1)$ to width
$q-1$, and let $\Lambda D$ be the join of the widening of
$\Lambda_0 E$ to width $\W(p,q)$ with the map whose cascade of
order~$0$ is the zero cascade and whose tail is $\Lambda D'$. At
order~$0$, the code $\act{E}{y \sqcup x}$ is below
$\bS(\act{E}{y \sqcup x}) \btimes \omega$, hence below the value at~$y$
of $\act{(\Lambda_0 E)}{x}$, and $\act{(\Lambda_0 E)}{x}$ is below the
first $p+1$ entries of $\act{(\Lambda D)}{x}$, by
\cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rjoin},\labelcref{item:rcut});
those entries are what the cascade of order~$0$ of
$\rd{\act{(\Lambda D)}{x}}$ is read off, and the value of a cascade is
monotone in its codes. At the orders above,
$\act{D'}{y' \sqcup x'} \tle \act{\rd{\act{(\Lambda D')}{x'}}}{y'}$
by induction, and $\act{(\Lambda D')}{x'}$ is below the tail of
$\act{(\Lambda D)}{x}$ by
\cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rjoin}), so monotonicity of the
reading concludes.

Iteration. By induction on~$n$. If $p = 0$ there is nothing to prove,
and $p \le n$ since $x \tsub u$, so we may take $n$ and~$p$ positive.
Write $u = (u_0, u')$, $x = (a, t)$ and $e = (e_0, e')$, and let
$E = (e_0, e')$ be the cascade of order~$0$ of~$\rd e$, which is read
off all $p+1$ entries of~$e$. The tail of $\rd e$ is $\rd{e'}$, a map
from width $p-1$ to itself, so the tail of the $j$-th iterate of
$\rd e$ at~$x$ is the $j$-th iterate of $\rd{e'}$ at~$t$. Since
$e' \tsub u'$ and $t \tsub u'$, the induction hypothesis gives a map
$R'$ from width $n-1$ to width $p-1$, depending on $n-1$ and $p-1$
alone, with all these tails below $D_t = \act{R'}{u'}$. Put
$T = \tsum D_t$ and $X = \act{e'}{D_t}$, the tuple $e'$ being read as
a cascade. The head $h_j$ of the $j$-th iterate satisfies $h_0 = a$
and
\[
  h_{j+1} = \big((h_j \bplus \tsum t_j) \bplus e_0\big) \btimes
    \omega^{\act{e'}{t_j}}
  \le \big(h_j \bplus (T \bplus e_0)\big) \btimes
    \omega^{(\bS X) \btimes \omega} ,
\]
where $t_j$ is the tail of the $j$-th iterate, by monotonicity,
associativity of addition and $X \le (\bS X) \btimes \omega$. By
\cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:iterate}) with $s = \bS X$, and
monotonicity of the map iterated there,
\[
  h_j \le \big(a \bplus (T \bplus e_0)\big) \btimes \omega^{B}
  \quad \text{for every } j, \qquad
  B = ((\bS X) \btimes \omega) \btimes \omega .
\]
It remains to put this code below the value at~$u$ of a cascade that
depends on $n$ and~$p$ alone. Let
$A' = (u_0 \bplus \tsum u') \bplus \num 1$, let
$V_T = \act{(\tsum R')}{u'}$, which is above~$T$
by~\cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rsum}), and let
$M = A' \btimes \omega^{V_T}$. The codes $a$ and~$e_0$ are below
$u_0$, hence below $A'$ and below~$M$, and $T \le V_T \le \omega^{V_T}
\le M$, so $a \bplus (T \bplus e_0)$ is below a finite multiple
of~$M$, and \cref{lem:absorb}(\labelcref{item:multiple},%
\labelcref{item:exp-law}) gives
\[
  \big(a \bplus (T \bplus e_0)\big) \btimes \omega^{B}
  \le M \btimes \omega^{\bS B}
  = A' \btimes \omega^{V_T \bplus \bS B} .
\]
Since $e' \tsub u'$, projection gives $e' \tle \act{\mathrm{zero}}{u'}$
for the zero map from width $n-1$ to width~$p$, and the first step of
application gives $X \le \act{A(\mathrm{zero}, R')}{u'}$, since $X$ is
below the value at $D_t$ of $\act{\mathrm{zero}}{u'}$ read as a
cascade, by monotonicity of the value in the codes,
\cref{lem:cascade-basic}. Applying
\cref{lem:cascade-ops}(\labelcref{item:suc},\labelcref{item:bump})
four times,
$\bS B \le \act{O_B}{u'}$ for the cascade
$O_B = (((A(\mathrm{zero}, R')^{+})^{\times})^{\times})^{+}$, and so
$V_T \bplus \bS B \le \act{O}{u'}$ for $O = \tsum R' \vee O_B$.
Therefore $h_j \le A' \btimes \omega^{\act O {u'}} =
\act{(\num 1, O)}{u}$ for every~$j$, and we put
$R = ((\num 1, O), R')$. The tails of the iterates are below
$\act{R'}{u'}$, which is the tail of $\act R u$.

Each construction preserves smallness,
by~\cref{lem:cascade-ops,lem:rect-ops}.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectApply}{ssh}; \texttt{valSc-ssh},
\texttt{ssh-All<}, \texttt{rect-preRD};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectCurry}{scurryD}, \texttt{valSc-scurry},
\texttt{tsum-cmb-⋖}, \texttt{scurryD-All<};
\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.RectOrbitAct}{rtail-iter}, \texttt{orbEI},
\texttt{orbEI-All<}, \texttt{rorbitD-All<}.}
\end{proof}

Part~(\labelcref{item:orbit}) is what makes the bound on the recursor
a datum rather than an existential tuple, since the bound on the
iterates is produced once and for all from the context.

\section{The bound on the majorant}
\label{sec:bound}

We define the predicate that a datum dominates a majorant and prove
the closure properties of it that the constants share, give the clause
for each constant, and conclude.

\subsection{The predicate}
\label{sec:predicate}

\begin{definition}[The order of a type and the domination predicate]
\label{def:dom}
The order of a type and the width of its data are
\[
  \ord(\gr) = 0 , \qquad
  \ord(\sigma\arr\tau) = \max(\ord(\sigma)+1, \ord(\tau)) ,
\]
\[
  \dwd(\sigma) = \ord(\sigma)+1 .
\]
A datum $d$ of width $\dwd(\sigma)$ \emph{dominates} a majorant $x$ of
type~$\sigma$, written $x \dom_{\sigma} d$, by recursion on the type:
at the ground type $a \dom_{\gr} d$ means $a \le \tsum d$, and
$F \dom_{\sigma\arr\tau} d$ means that
$x \dom_{\sigma} e$ implies $F\,x \dom_{\tau} \act{\rd d}{e}$ for
every small~$e$, where $\rd d$ is the graded map from width
$\dwd(\sigma)$ to width $\dwd(\tau)$ read off~$d$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Predicate}{ord}; \texttt{wd},
\texttt{Dom}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The widths agree]
\label{lem:widths}
For all types $\sigma$ and~$\tau$,
$\dwd(\sigma\arr\tau) = \W(\dwd(\sigma), \dwd(\tau))$.
\AgdaRefs{The two sides are definitionally equal, which is what lets
the arrow clause of \Agda{DialogueTrees.Predicate}{Dom} typecheck
with no coercion, so there is no separate lemma to cite.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both sides are $\max(\ord(\sigma)+1, \ord(\tau))+1$, by the clause
$\max(m+1,n+1) = \max(m,n)+1$.
\end{proof}

The grading of the types and the grading of the data therefore agree
exactly, and the clause for an arrow reads the datum as a map and
applies it with no padding.

\begin{lemma}[Closure properties]
\label{lem:dom-closure}
Let $\sigma$ be a type.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:dom-mono}
  The predicate is monotone: if $x \dom_{\sigma} d$ and
  $d \tle d'$ then $x \dom_{\sigma} d'$.
\item\label{item:dom-sup}
  It is closed under pointwise suprema: if $h\,n \dom_{\sigma} d$ for
  every~$n$, then $\sup_{\sigma} h \dom_{\sigma} d$.
\item\label{item:dom-add}
  It is closed under the translation: if $x \dom_{\sigma} d$ and
  $\nu \le \nu'$, then
  $x \bplus_{\sigma} \nu \dom_{\sigma} d^{+\nu'}$, the datum
  of~\cref{def:rect-ops} that adds $\nu'$ at order~$0$ and takes a
  successor at every order above.
\item\label{item:dom-eps}
  At the ground type a small datum gives a bound below~$\eps$: if
  $a \dom_{\gr} d$ and $d$ is small then $a < \eps$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Predicate}{Dom-mono};
\texttt{Dom-sup},
\texttt{Dom-⊕}, \texttt{Dom-<-ε₀}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At the ground type a datum is a single code~$d_0$, with
$\tsum d = d_0$, so that $a \dom_{\gr} d$ means $a \le d_0$.

Parts (\labelcref{item:dom-mono})
to~(\labelcref{item:dom-add}) are inductions on the type whose arrow
case passes under the argument. At the ground type,
(\labelcref{item:dom-mono}) is transitivity,
(\labelcref{item:dom-sup}) is the limit rule, and
(\labelcref{item:dom-add}) is $a \bplus \nu \le d_0 \bplus \nu'$,
which follows from $a \le d_0$ and $\nu \le \nu'$
by~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:add-mono}). The arrow case
of~(\labelcref{item:dom-mono}) uses monotonicity of the reading and of
the action in the map, that of~(\labelcref{item:dom-sup}) passes the
supremum under the argument on both sides, and that
of~(\labelcref{item:dom-add}) uses
$(\act{\rd d}{e})^{+\nu'} \tle \act{\rd{d^{+\nu'}}}{e}$,
from~\cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rread}),
and~(\labelcref{item:dom-mono}). For~(\labelcref{item:dom-add}), a
translation alone would not do: absorbing a code into a translation
under a limit multiplier asks for
$b \btimes m \bplus d \le (b \bplus d) \btimes m$, which is false, so
the code is added at order~$0$ and a successor is taken at every order
above.

Part~(\labelcref{item:dom-eps}) is not an induction. From $a \le d_0$
and $d_0 < \eps$, that is $\bS d_0 \le \eps$, the successor rule and
transitivity give $\bS a \le \bS d_0 \le \eps$, that is $a < \eps$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The clauses}
\label{sec:clauses}

\begin{lemma}[The easy clauses]
\label{lem:easy-clauses}
Any datum dominates the majorants of $\Zeroc$, $\Succc$ and~$\Kc$, and
the datum $(\bS\,\bZ, s)$ dominates the majorant of the oracle for
every~$s$. Application is the clause for an arrow read forwards.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Predicate}{Zero-clause};
\texttt{Succ-clause}, \texttt{Ω-clause}, \texttt{K-clause},
\texttt{app-clause}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The clause for $\Zeroc$ is the least element property of~$\bZ$, and
the one for $\Succc$ holds because a datum is below its image under a
graded map between equal widths, each output being the value of a
cascade at the suffix of the datum from its own order,
by~\cref{lem:cascade-basic}. The oracle adds one query and therefore
asks that the slot of order~$0$ be positive, which
$\bS\,\bZ$ supplies. For~$\Kc$, whose majorant discards its second
argument, the datum of the first argument has to survive two
applications; the output of the first application still dominates the
datum it was given, and the output of the second still dominates the
datum it was read from, and composing the two under monotonicity is
the whole proof.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{BrouwerOrdinals.Rect}{rect-increasing};
\texttt{rect-arg-≥}, \texttt{rect-rdOf-≥}.}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The clause for $\Sc$]
\label{lem:s-clause}
At every triple of types there is a small datum that dominates the
majorant of~$\Sc$, depending only on the three widths.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.SClause}{dS}; \texttt{dS-All<},
\texttt{S-clause}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The datum is built rather than designed, from~\cref{lem:calculus},
and the proof is the one a reader of $\lambda f\,g\,a.\,f\,a\,(g\,a)$
would write. Let the types be $\rho\arr\sigma\arr\tau$,
$\rho\arr\sigma$ and~$\rho$, let $m$, $p$ and~$q$ be the widths of
the data of $\rho$, $\sigma$ and~$\tau$, and let
$\varphi \dom d_\varphi$, $\gamma \dom d_\gamma$ and $a \dom e$ for small data
$d_\varphi$, $d_\gamma$ and~$e$. The three data are combined into the
small datum $u = e \sqcup u_2$ with $u_2 = d_\gamma \sqcup d_\varphi$,
the variable bound last coming first, and each of them sits
inside~$u$. By projection
and~\cref{lem:dom-closure}(\labelcref{item:dom-mono}), $\varphi$, $\gamma$ and~$a$ are dominated by the
images of~$u$ under zero maps of the appropriate widths, and three
applications, each the clause for an arrow followed by
\cref{lem:calculus}(\labelcref{item:apply}), give
\[
  \varphi\,a \dom \act{A_1}{u} , \qquad
  \gamma\,a \dom \act{A_2}{u} , \qquad
  \varphi\,a\,(\gamma\,a) \dom \act{B}{u} ,
\]
where $A_1 = \mathrm{zero}\cdot\mathrm{zero}$ goes to width
$\W(p,q)$, $A_2 = \mathrm{zero}\cdot\mathrm{zero}$ to width~$p$ and
$B = A_1 \cdot A_2$ to width~$q$. The three binders are abstraction
twice and flattening:
\begin{gather*}
  \act{B}{e \sqcup u_2} \tle \act{\rd{\act{(\Lambda B)}{u_2}}}{e} ,
  \qquad
  \act{(\Lambda B)}{d_\gamma \sqcup d_\varphi}
    \tle \act{\rd{\act{(\Lambda\Lambda B)}{d_\varphi}}}{d_\gamma} ,
  \\
  \Lambda\Lambda B \tle \rd{d_S} ,
\end{gather*}
for $d_S = \flat\Lambda\Lambda B$, by
\cref{lem:calculus}(\labelcref{item:curry})
and~\cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rflat}). By monotonicity of
the reading and of the action in the map,
\[
  \act{B}{u} \tle
  \act{\rd{\act{\rd{\act{\rd{d_S}}{d_\varphi}}}{d_\gamma}}}{e} ,
\]
and the right-hand side is the datum that three clauses for an arrow
assign to $\varphi\,a\,(\gamma\,a)$, the value of the majorant of~$\Sc$
at $\varphi$, $\gamma$ and~$a$, when $d_S$ is taken as the datum
of~$\Sc$. So $d_S$ dominates the majorant of~$\Sc$
by~\cref{lem:dom-closure}(\labelcref{item:dom-mono}). The datum $d_S$ is built from zero maps
by application, abstraction and flattening, so it is small and depends
only on $m$, $p$ and~$q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The clause for the recursor]
\label{lem:iter-clause}
At every type there is a small datum that dominates the majorant of
the recursor.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.IterClause}{dI}; \texttt{dI-All<},
\texttt{Iter-clause}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $p$ be the width of the data of~$\sigma$, and let
$\varphi \dom d_\varphi$, $a \dom d_a$ and $\nu \dom d_\nu$ for small
data of widths $\W(p,p)$, $p$ and~$1$, with $d_\nu = (v)$, so that
$\nu \le v$. Combine them into the small datum
$u = d_\nu \sqcup (d_a \sqcup d_\varphi)$, the variable bound last
coming first, whose first entry is above~$v$. The data
$d_j = \rd{d_\varphi}^{\,j}(d_a)$ are small and satisfy
$\iterop\,\varphi\,a\,j \dom d_j$, by induction on~$j$ through the
clause for an arrow, and since $d_\varphi$ and $d_a$ sit inside~$u$,
\cref{lem:calculus}(\labelcref{item:orbit}) gives a map~$R$, depending
on the widths alone, with $d_j \tle \act R u$ for every~$j$.
By~\cref{lem:dom-closure}(\labelcref{item:dom-mono},%
\labelcref{item:dom-sup}),
$\sup_{\sigma}(j \mapsto \iterop\,\varphi\,a\,j) \dom \act R u$, and
by~(\labelcref{item:dom-add}) of the same lemma the majorant
\[
  \Mj_{\Iterc}\,\varphi\,a\,\nu
  = \big(\textstyle\sup_{\sigma}(j \mapsto \iterop\,\varphi\,a\,j)\big)
    \bplus_{\sigma} \nu
\]
is dominated by $(\act R u)^{+v}$, which is below
$\act{R^{\dagger}}{u}$ by~\cref{lem:rect-ops}(\labelcref{item:rtrans}).
The map $R^{\dagger}$ does not depend on~$v$, the code added belonging
to the context as well. The three binders are then abstraction twice
and flattening, exactly as in~\cref{lem:s-clause}, and give the datum
$d_I = \flat\Lambda\Lambda R^{\dagger}$ of the recursor, which is
small and depends only on~$p$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The theorem}
\label{sec:theorem}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:fundamental}
Every closed term $t$ of $\TmO\sigma$ has a small datum of width
$\dwd(\sigma)$ dominating its majorant.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Fundamental}{fundamental}.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the term, the constants by~\cref{lem:easy-clauses},
\cref{lem:s-clause} and~\cref{lem:iter-clause}, and application by the
clause for an arrow, whose datum is the image of the datum of the
argument under the datum of the function read as a graded map, and is
small by~\cref{lem:cascade-basic}, each of its entries being the value
of a small cascade at a small datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:mu-below}
Every closed term $t$ of $\TmO\gr$ has $\Mj(t) < \eps$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Fundamental}{μᵀ-<-ε₀}.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At the ground type the datum is a single code, so
\cref{lem:dom-closure}(\labelcref{item:dom-eps}) applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The upper bound]
\label{thm:main}
For every closed term $t : (\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ of system T,
\[
  \height(\dtree(t)) < \eps .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Fundamental}{dialogue-tree-height-<-ε₀};
\Agda{DialogueTrees.Main}{restated}.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{cor:mu-below} discharges the hypothesis of
\cref{thm:reduction}.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharpness}
\label{sec:sharp}

We exhibit terms of small height, read a dialogue tree off every
Brouwer code, and construct for each~$n$ a term attaining the
approximant $\tower n$, from which $\eps$ is the least bound.

\subsection{Terms of small height}
\label{sec:small}

\begin{examples}
\label{ex:small}
The three terms
$\lambda f.\,0$, $\lambda f.\,f\,0$ and
$\lambda f.\,\iterop\,f\,(f\,0)\,(f\,0)$ have dialogue trees
$\eta\,0$, $\beta\,\eta\,0$ and
$\beta(j \mapsto \iterop\,\mathrm{generic}\,(\beta\,\eta\,0)\,j)\,0$,
each by computation. The first has height~$\bZ$, and the second has
height~$\bS\,\bZ$ in the sense that both bounds hold, the code
$\bL(n \mapsto \bS\,\bZ)$ being the one the definition returns.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Examples}{leaf-term};
\texttt{query-term}, \texttt{iterated-term}, \texttt{leaf-tree},
\texttt{query-tree}, \texttt{iterated-tree}, \texttt{leaf-height},
\texttt{query-height-≤}, \texttt{query-height-≥}.}
\end{examples}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:omega-attained}
The third term of~\cref{ex:small} has height~$\omega$, which is
$\tower 0$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Examples}{iterated-height-≥};
\texttt{iterated-height-≤}.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the computation of the tree in~\cref{ex:small}, the height is the
supremum over~$j$ of one more than the height of the $j$-th iterate of
the generic oracle at $\beta\,\eta\,0$. If the numeral $\num k$ is
below the height of a tree~$d$, then $\num{k+1}$ is below the height
of $\mathrm{generic}\,d$, by induction on~$k$ for all~$d$, the numeral
being carried through a single branch at each step as
in~\cref{rem:one-branch}. For $k = 0$, the tree
$\mathrm{generic}\,d$ is a query node by~\cref{def:kleisli-generic},
so its height is a limit whose members are successors, and $\num 1$ is
below it by the successor rule and the cocone at~$0$. For $k+1$, the tree $d$ is not a leaf,
since a leaf has height~$\bZ$ and no rule concludes
$\bS\,\num k \le \bZ$, so $d = \beta\,\varphi\,i$ and
$\height(d) = \bL(n \mapsto \bS\,\height(\varphi\,n))$. A derivation
of $\bS\,\num k \le \height(d)$ ends with the cocone, from
$\bS\,\num k \le \bS\,\height(\varphi\,n)$ for some~$n$, and this
ends with the successor rule, from
$\num k \le \height(\varphi\,n)$. By induction,
$\num{k+1} \le \height(\mathrm{generic}\,(\varphi\,n))$, so
$\num{k+2} \le \bS\,\height(\mathrm{generic}\,(\varphi\,n))$, which is
the member at~$n$ of
$\height(\mathrm{generic}\,d)
 = \bL(m \mapsto \bS\,\height(\mathrm{generic}\,(\varphi\,m)))$, and
the cocone concludes. So $\num j$ is below the height of the $j$-th
iterate, by induction on~$j$, which gives the lower bound. In the
other direction the $j$-th iterate has height at most
$\height(\beta\,\eta\,0) \bplus \num j$,
by~\cref{lem:height-three}(\labelcref{item:generic}) applied $j$~times,
which is below $\num 1 \bplus \num j = \num{j+1}$ by~\cref{ex:small}
and~\cref{lem:add}(\labelcref{item:add-mono}), so every branch is
below~$\omega$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Examples}{ι-generic};
\texttt{ι-iter},
\texttt{iter-generic-≤}.}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Trees of arbitrary height}
\label{sec:trees}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:tree-of}
Every Brouwer code is below the height of a dialogue tree read off the
code. In particular $\eps$ is below the height of such a tree, and
that tree is the dialogue tree of no term of system T.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Examples}{tree-of};
\texttt{code-≤-height}, \texttt{approximant}, \texttt{epsilon₀},
\texttt{no-term-of-height-ε₀}.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A successor becomes a query whose subtrees are all the tree of the
predecessor, and a limit becomes a query whose $n$-th subtree is the
tree of the $n$-th member, and $a \le \height(\mathrm{tree}(a))$
follows by induction on~$a$. Were a term~$t$ to have the tree of~$\eps$,
then $\eps \le \height(\dtree(t))$, so that
$\bS\,\eps \le \bS\,\height(\dtree(t)) \le \eps$ by monotonicity of
the successor and \cref{thm:main}, and $\bS\,\eps \le \eps$ is
excluded by~\cref{lem:irrefl}.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Examples}{S-irrefl}.}
\end{proof}

The bound of~\cref{thm:main} is thus a statement about the terms, the
trees themselves reaching every height.

\subsection{Terms attaining each approximant}
\label{sec:church}

The terms of this section are Church encodings of ordinals, following
Coquand, Hancock and Setzer~\cite{CoquandHancockSetzer1997}; the same
device is applied to dialogue trees, which are Church encoded within
system T, in~\cite{EscardoPaivaRahliTosun2025}.

\begin{definition}[Church ordinals in system T]
\label{def:church}
A Church ordinal over a type~$\sigma$ folds itself over an
interpretation of zero, successor and limit, so it inhabits
\[
  \mathcal{O}\sigma = \sigma \arr (\sigma\arr\sigma)
    \arr ((\gr\arr\sigma)\arr\sigma) \arr \sigma .
\]
Zero, successor, limit and addition are the terms
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{zer} &= \lambda z\,s\,l.\,z , &
  \mathrm{suc}\,c &= \lambda z\,s\,l.\,s\,(c\,z\,s\,l) , \\
  \mathrm{lim}\,f &= \lambda z\,s\,l.\,l\,(\lambda n.\,f\,n\,z\,s\,l) , &
  \mathrm{add}\,a\,b &= \lambda z\,s\,l.\,a\,(b\,z\,s\,l)\,s\,l ,
\end{align*}
which keep the parameter~$\sigma$ fixed, while multiplication and
exponentiation consume an encoding one level up,
\[
  \mathrm{mul}\,a\,b
    = b\,\mathrm{zer}\,(\lambda r.\,\mathrm{add}\,r\,a)\,\mathrm{lim} ,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{exp}\,a\,b
    = b\,(\mathrm{suc}\,\mathrm{zer})\,(\lambda r.\,\mathrm{mul}\,r\,a)
      \,\mathrm{lim} ,
\]
with $\mathrm{mul} : \mathcal{O}\sigma \arr \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{O}\sigma)
 \arr \mathcal{O}\sigma$ and
$\mathrm{exp} : \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{O}\sigma) \arr
 \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{O}\sigma) \arr \mathcal{O}\sigma$. The numerals
form the sequence
$\mathrm{fin} = \Iterc\,\mathrm{suc}\,\mathrm{zer} :
 \gr \arr \mathcal{O}\sigma$, its limit
$\omega^{\Tm} = \mathrm{lim}\,\mathrm{fin}$ encodes~$\omega$, and the
approximants are encoded by
\[
  \mathrm{tower}_0 = \omega^{\Tm} , \qquad
  \mathrm{tower}_{n+1} = \mathrm{exp}\,\omega^{\Tm}\,\mathrm{tower}_n ,
\]
where $\mathrm{tower}_{n+1}$ at the type $\mathcal{O}\sigma$ uses
$\omega^{\Tm}$ and $\mathrm{tower}_n$ at the type
$\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{O}\sigma)$. The approximant $\tower n$ is
therefore written at its own type, with $n$ nestings
of~$\mathcal{O}$ below the level at which it is used.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{𝓞}; \texttt{zerᵀ},
\texttt{sucᵀ}, \texttt{limᵀ}, \texttt{addᵀ}, \texttt{mulᵀ},
\texttt{expᵀ}, \texttt{finᵀ}, \texttt{ωᵀ}, \texttt{towerᵀ};
\Agda{Ordinals.ChurchEncoding}{O}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:no-eps}
Every approximant of~$\eps$ is written in system T in this way, while
$\eps$ itself calls for a uniform type and stays out of reach, in
accordance with~\cref{thm:main}. Dependent types and a universe give
the uniform typing and hence~$\eps$, which is how
\cite{CoquandHancockSetzer1997} obtains it.
\end{remark}

The codes that the encodings denote are computed by folds.

\begin{definition}[Folds of codes]
\label{def:fold}
For a code~$z'$, a map~$s'$ from codes to codes and a map~$l'$ from
sequences of codes to codes, the fold
$\mathrm{fold}_{z',s',l'}$ is the map on codes defined by
\[
  \mathrm{fold}(\bZ) = z' , \qquad
  \mathrm{fold}(\bS a) = s'(\mathrm{fold}(a)) , \qquad
  \mathrm{fold}(\bL f) = l'(n \mapsto \mathrm{fold}(f\,n)) .
\]
We write
\[
  \mathrm{mul}^{c}(a, b)
    = \mathrm{fold}_{\bZ,\, r \mapsto a \bplus r,\, \bL}(b) ,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{exp}^{c}(a, b)
    = \mathrm{fold}_{\num 1,\, r \mapsto \mathrm{mul}^{c}(r, a),\,
      \bL}(b) ,
\]
and
$\mathrm{tower}^{c}_0 = \omega$ and
$\mathrm{tower}^{c}_{n+1} = \mathrm{exp}^{c}(\omega,
 \mathrm{tower}^{c}_n)$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{fold}; \texttt{mulᶜ},
\texttt{expᶜ}, \texttt{towerᶜ}.}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The folds]
\label{lem:fold}
For all codes $a$, $b$ and~$x$,
\[
  \mathrm{fold}_{\bZ,\bS,\bL}(a) = a , \qquad
  \mathrm{fold}_{z',s',l'}(a \bplus b)
    = \mathrm{fold}_{\mathrm{fold}_{z',s',l'}(a),\, s',\, l'}(b) ,
\]
and $\mathrm{mul}^{c}(x, \omega) = x \btimes \omega$ and
$\mathrm{exp}^{c}(\omega, b) = \omega^{b}$, so that
$\mathrm{tower}^{c}_n = \tower n$ for every~$n$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{fold-id}; \texttt{fold-⊕},
\texttt{mulᶜ-ι}, \texttt{mulᶜ-ω}, \texttt{expᶜ-ω\^{}},
\texttt{towerᶜ-tower}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two identities are inductions on $a$ and on~$b$, using function
extensionality at a limit. The fold $\mathrm{mul}^{c}$ groups its
summands in the other order from the multiplication
of~\cref{def:arithmetic}, and
$\mathrm{mul}^{c}(x, \num n) = x \btimes \num n$ by induction on~$n$,
since $x \bplus x \btimes \num n = x \btimes \num n \bplus x$ by
associativity of addition; both sides of
$\mathrm{mul}^{c}(x, \omega) = x \btimes \omega$ are the limits of
these. The identity for $\mathrm{exp}^{c}$ is an induction on~$b$,
using the one for $\mathrm{mul}^{c}$ at a successor, and the last
claim follows by induction on~$n$.
\end{proof}

In the dialogue interpretation a value of type $\gr \arr \sigma$ is a
function from dialogue trees to $\dsem\sigma$, and the interpretation
of a term written in $\lambda$-notation computes as the
$\lambda$-term reads, since $\Kc$ and~$\Sc$ are interpreted as the
projection and the substitution. Two further facts are used below.
The recursor is interpreted as a Kleisli extension
(\cref{def:dialogue-interpretation}), so that
$\dsem{\mathrm{fin}} = \kext{(k \mapsto
 \dsem{\mathrm{suc}}^{k}\,\dsem{\mathrm{zer}})}$. And the Kleisli
extension at an arrow type acts under the argument, so that for
$g : \N \to \dsem{\mathcal{O}\sigma}$ and values $z$, $s$, $l$,
\[
  (\kext g\,d)\,z\,s\,l = \kext{(k \mapsto g\,k\,z\,s\,l)}\,d ,
\]
and hence
\[
  \dsem{\mathrm{lim}}\,(\kext g)\,z\,s\,l
    = l\,\big(\kext{(k \mapsto g\,k\,z\,s\,l)}\big) .
\]

\begin{definition}[The parametric relation]
\label{def:rel}
Let $\sigma$ be a type and $R$ a relation between $\dsem\sigma$ and
the Brouwer codes. A value $c$ of $\dsem{\mathcal{O}\sigma}$ and a
code~$a$ satisfy $\mathrm{Rel}_{\sigma,R}(c, a)$ when
\[
  R\big(c\,z\,s\,l,\ \mathrm{fold}_{z',s',l'}(a)\big)
\]
for all values $z$ of $\dsem\sigma$, maps
$s : \dsem\sigma \to \dsem\sigma$ and
$l : (\dsem\gr \to \dsem\sigma) \to \dsem\sigma$, and all $z'$, $s'$
and~$l'$ as in~\cref{def:fold}, such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:rel-z}
  $R(z, z')$,
\item\label{item:rel-s}
  $R(x, y)$ implies $R(s\,x, s'\,y)$ for all $x$ and~$y$, and
\item\label{item:rel-l}
  for all $g : \N \to \dsem\sigma$ and sequences of codes~$h$ with
  $R(g\,j, h\,j)$ for every~$j$, we have $R(l\,(\kext g), l'\,h)$,
\end{enumerate}
where $\kext g$ is the Kleisli extension at type~$\sigma$
of~\cref{sec:dialogue}.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{Rel}.}
\end{definition}

Condition~(\labelcref{item:rel-l}) asks about the limit only at
Kleisli extensions $\kext g$, which branch over the answer to a query
and continue with $g\,j$ on the $j$-th branch. A function on trees
that ignores its argument makes no query, and the condition says
nothing about it. The terms of~\cref{def:church} pass to a limit
nothing but Kleisli extensions, which is what the following lemma
uses.

\begin{lemma}[Preservation of the relation]
\label{lem:rel}
Let $\sigma$ be a type and $R$ a relation between $\dsem\sigma$ and
the Brouwer codes, and write $\mathrm{Rel}$ for
$\mathrm{Rel}_{\sigma,R}$ and $\mathrm{Rel}'$ for
$\mathrm{Rel}_{\mathcal{O}\sigma,\mathrm{Rel}}$, the relation one level
up whose parameter relation is $\mathrm{Rel}$ itself.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item:p-zs}
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\mathrm{zer}}, \bZ)$, and
  $\mathrm{Rel}(c, a)$ implies
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\mathrm{suc}}\,c, \bS a)$.
\item\label{item:p-lim}
  If $\mathrm{Rel}(g\,j, h\,j)$ for every~$j$, then
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\mathrm{lim}}\,(\kext g), \bL h)$.
\item\label{item:p-add}
  If $\mathrm{Rel}(a, a')$ and $\mathrm{Rel}(b, b')$, then
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\mathrm{add}}\,a\,b, b' \bplus a')$.
\item\label{item:p-mul}
  If $\mathrm{Rel}(a, a')$ and $\mathrm{Rel}'(b, b')$, then
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\mathrm{mul}}\,a\,b, \mathrm{mul}^{c}(a', b'))$.
\item\label{item:p-exp}
  If $\mathrm{Rel}'(a, a')$ and $\mathrm{Rel}'(b, b')$, then
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\mathrm{exp}}\,a\,b, \mathrm{exp}^{c}(a', b'))$.
\item\label{item:p-tower}
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\omega^{\Tm}}, \omega)$, and
  $\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\mathrm{tower}_n}, \mathrm{tower}^{c}_n)$ for
  every~$n$.
\end{enumerate}
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{zerᵀ-Rel}; \texttt{sucᵀ-Rel},
\texttt{limᵀ-Rel}, \texttt{addᵀ-Rel}, \texttt{mulᵀ-Rel},
\texttt{expᵀ-Rel}, \texttt{finᵀ-Rel}, \texttt{ωᵀ-Rel},
\texttt{towerᵀ-Rel}.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the first three parts, fix $z$, $s$, $l$, $z'$, $s'$ and~$l'$
satisfying the three conditions of~\cref{def:rel}, and write
$\mathrm{fold}$ for $\mathrm{fold}_{z',s',l'}$.

(\labelcref{item:p-zs}) The first claim is
condition~(\labelcref{item:rel-z}), and the second follows from
condition~(\labelcref{item:rel-s}), since
$\dsem{\mathrm{suc}}\,c\,z\,s\,l = s\,(c\,z\,s\,l)$ and
$\mathrm{fold}(\bS a) = s'(\mathrm{fold}(a))$.

(\labelcref{item:p-lim}) By the second fact above,
$\dsem{\mathrm{lim}}\,(\kext g)\,z\,s\,l
 = l\,(\kext{(k \mapsto g\,k\,z\,s\,l)})$. The sequences
$k \mapsto g\,k\,z\,s\,l$ and $k \mapsto \mathrm{fold}(h\,k)$ are
related pointwise by hypothesis, so
condition~(\labelcref{item:rel-l}) relates the value to
$l'(k \mapsto \mathrm{fold}(h\,k)) = \mathrm{fold}(\bL h)$.

(\labelcref{item:p-add}) We have
$\dsem{\mathrm{add}}\,a\,b\,z\,s\,l = a\,(b\,z\,s\,l)\,s\,l$. The
hypothesis on~$b$ gives $R(b\,z\,s\,l, \mathrm{fold}(b'))$, so the
hypothesis on~$a$, applied with $b\,z\,s\,l$ and $\mathrm{fold}(b')$
in place of $z$ and~$z'$, relates the value to
$\mathrm{fold}_{\mathrm{fold}(b'),s',l'}(a')$, which is
$\mathrm{fold}(b' \bplus a')$ by~\cref{lem:fold}.

(\labelcref{item:p-mul}) We have
\begin{align*}
  \dsem{\mathrm{mul}}\,a\,b &= b\,\dsem{\mathrm{zer}}\,
    (r \mapsto \dsem{\mathrm{add}}\,r\,a)\,\dsem{\mathrm{lim}} , \\
  \mathrm{mul}^{c}(a', b')
    &= \mathrm{fold}_{\bZ,\, r \mapsto a' \bplus r,\, \bL}(b') .
\end{align*}
So the claim is the hypothesis $\mathrm{Rel}'(b, b')$
at these two algebras, whose three conditions, for the relation
$\mathrm{Rel}$, are (\labelcref{item:p-zs}), (\labelcref{item:p-add})
and~(\labelcref{item:p-lim}). The third condition is met because
$\mathrm{Rel}'$ asks it only at Kleisli extensions, which is what
(\labelcref{item:p-lim}) handles.

(\labelcref{item:p-exp}) In the same way,
\begin{align*}
  \dsem{\mathrm{exp}}\,a\,b &= b\,(\dsem{\mathrm{suc}}\,
    \dsem{\mathrm{zer}})\,(r \mapsto \dsem{\mathrm{mul}}\,r\,a)\,
    \dsem{\mathrm{lim}} , \\
  \mathrm{exp}^{c}(a', b')
    &= \mathrm{fold}_{\num 1,\, r \mapsto \mathrm{mul}^{c}(r, a'),\,
      \bL}(b') ,
\end{align*}
and the three conditions are
(\labelcref{item:p-zs}), (\labelcref{item:p-mul})
and~(\labelcref{item:p-lim}).

(\labelcref{item:p-tower}) By the first fact above,
\[
  \dsem{\omega^{\Tm}} = \dsem{\mathrm{lim}}\,\big(\kext{(k \mapsto
    \dsem{\mathrm{suc}}^{k}\,\dsem{\mathrm{zer}})}\big) ,
\]
and the values $\dsem{\mathrm{suc}}^{k}\,\dsem{\mathrm{zer}}$ and
$\num k$ satisfy $\mathrm{Rel}$ by~(\labelcref{item:p-zs}) and
induction on~$k$, so
(\labelcref{item:p-lim}) gives
$\mathrm{Rel}(\dsem{\omega^{\Tm}}, \bL(k \mapsto \num k))$, and the
limit is~$\omega$. The second claim is proved by induction on~$n$ for
all $\sigma$ and~$R$ at once. At $n+1$, the terms $\omega^{\Tm}$ and
$\mathrm{tower}_n$ at the type $\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{O}\sigma)$ are
related by $\mathrm{Rel}'$ to $\omega$ and $\mathrm{tower}^{c}_n$, by
the first claim and the induction hypothesis at
$\mathcal{O}\sigma$ and $\mathrm{Rel}$, and (\labelcref{item:p-exp})
concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The terms]
\label{def:tower-term}
The term $t_{n} : (\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ is
\[
  t_n = \lambda\alpha.\,\mathrm{tower}_n\,\Zeroc\,
    (\lambda x.\,\Iterc\,I\,x\,(\alpha\,\Zeroc))\,
    (\lambda\varphi.\,\varphi\,(\alpha\,\Zeroc)) ,
\]
with $\mathrm{tower}_n$ at the ground type. It runs the encoding of
$\tower n$ against the oracle~$\alpha$ through the algebra that
interprets zero as~$0$, the successor of~$x$ as
$\iterop\,(\lambda y.\,y)\,x\,(\alpha\,0)$, and the limit of a
sequence~$\varphi$ as $\varphi\,(\alpha\,0)$.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{tower-term}; \texttt{abs},
\texttt{tm}.}
\end{definition}

In the standard interpretation the successor of the algebra is the
identity, since iterating the identity returns~$x$ whatever the count.
What the dialogue interpretation makes of it is another matter,
because the recursor reads its count through a Kleisli extension, so
the tree of the count is placed above the tree of~$x$. The successor
therefore places one query above the tree built so far: the oracle is
asked at~$0$ and every branch of the answer returns~$x$, so the
height of the result exceeds the height of~$x$ by one. An
interpretation of the successor that asks the oracle below, as the
term of~\cref{prop:omega-attained} does, raises every leaf by one and
leaves the height of an infinite tree where it was, which is why the
query goes on top. The limit hands its query to the sequence, and the
sequence branches over the answer, with the tree of the $j$-th member
on the $j$-th branch.

\begin{theorem}[The lower bound]
\label{thm:lower}
For every~$n$,
\[
  \tower n \le \height(\dtree(t_{n})) .
\]
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{tower-attained};
\Agda{DialogueTrees.Main}{attained}.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By parametricity. The dialogue tree of~$t_n$ is
$\dsem{\mathrm{tower}_n}\,z\,s\,l$ with
\[
  z = \eta\,0 , \qquad
  s\,x = \kext{(k \mapsto x)}\,(\beta\,\eta\,0)
       = \beta\,(k \mapsto x)\,0 , \qquad
  l\,\varphi = \varphi\,(\beta\,\eta\,0) ,
\]
since $\alpha\,\Zeroc$ is interpreted as
$\mathrm{generic}\,(\eta\,0) = \beta\,\eta\,0$, and the recursor at the
identity and~$x$ as the Kleisli extension of
$k \mapsto \iterop\,(\lambda y.\,y)\,x\,k = x$. We
apply~\cref{lem:rel}(\labelcref{item:p-tower}) at the ground type,
with $R(d, a)$ meaning $a \le \height(d)$, to these three values and
to the code algebra $\bZ$, $\bS$, $\bL$, after checking the three
conditions of~\cref{def:rel}.
Condition~(\labelcref{item:rel-z}) is $\bZ \le \height(\eta\,0)$. For
condition~(\labelcref{item:rel-s}), if $y \le \height(x)$ then
$\bS y \le \bS\,\height(x) \le \bL(k \mapsto \bS\,\height(x))
 = \height(s\,x)$, by the successor rule and the cocone rule. For
condition~(\labelcref{item:rel-l}), if $h\,j \le \height(g\,j)$ for
every~$j$, then
$l\,(\kext g) = \kext g\,(\beta\,\eta\,0) = \beta\,g\,0$, whose height
$\bL(j \mapsto \bS\,\height(g\,j))$ is above every $h\,j$, so
$\bL h \le \height(l\,(\kext g))$ by the limit rule. The lemma gives
$\mathrm{fold}_{\bZ,\bS,\bL}(\mathrm{tower}^{c}_n) \le
\height(\dtree(t_n))$, and the left-hand side is
$\mathrm{tower}^{c}_n = \tower n$ by~\cref{lem:fold}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:least}
The heights of the dialogue trees of closed terms of type
$(\gr\arr\gr)\arr\gr$ are cofinal in~$\eps$, and $\eps$ is the least
code bounding all of them.
\AgdaRefs{\Agda{DialogueTrees.Sharpness}{heights-cofinal};
\texttt{ε₀-least}, \texttt{tower-term-below-ε₀};
\Agda{DialogueTrees.Main}{cofinal}, \texttt{least}.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A code below~$\eps$ is below some $\tower n$, which
\cref{thm:lower} attains. If $b$ bounds the height of every tree then
$\tower n \le b$ for every~$n$, so $\eps \le b$ by the limiting
property of the order.
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We place the bound and the method in the literature, and compare the
proof with that of Xiao and Sun.

\subsection{The ordinal and the method}
\label{sec:method}

The bound $\eps$ is the proof-theoretic ordinal of arithmetic, by
Gentzen~\cite{Gentzen1936}; a modern account is
Pohlers~\cite{Pohlers2009}. The hereditary assignment of ordinals to
terms, type by type, is the method of Howard~\cite{Howard1970}, who
bounds the values a term computes and the length of its reductions.
The dialogue-tree height is a different measure, and
\cref{thm:main} is not a corollary of that assignment, but the shape
of the induction is the same.

The present proof has two features independent of the result. The
first is that the ordinals are Brouwer codes. With their natural
order the successor is monotone by construction, which is what allows every lemma to be proved without
excluded middle; a treatment through the ordinals of the univalent
library needs it, because the successor on those ordinals is not
monotone without it. The second
is the grading. The predicate of~\cref{def:dom} is indexed by the
order of a type, and~\cref{lem:widths} says that this grading is the
same as the grading of the data, so that the clause for an arrow
applies a graded map with no adjustment. Once that is in place the two
hard clauses are written in the calculus of~\cref{sec:graded} rather
than by hand.

\subsection{The proof of Xiao and Sun}
\label{sec:xiaosun}

The bound of \cref{thm:main} is due to Xiao and
Sun~\cite{XiaoSun2026}, and the proof given here is an alternative
one. Their argument runs through the syntax. A recursor is translated
into a closed infinitary template, the redexes are removed by a
sequence of passes indexed by ordinary type level, the translation and
every pass preserve the dialogue denotation, and an auxiliary rank
with an additive substitution bound is tracked across the passes. The
outcome is sharper than \cref{thm:main} in the respect that matters
for a given term: they compute from the term a natural
number~$K(t) \ge 2$ with
\[
  \height(\dtree(t)) < \tower{K(t)} < \eps ,
\]
so each term is bounded by a named level of the tower rather than
by~$\eps$. The proof here names no such level.

The two proofs differ in their ordinals. Theirs are the classical
ones, and the ordinal part of their formalization assumes excluded
middle for the propositions of the second universe, together with
univalence, propositional truncation and set replacement, while their
syntactic part assumes nothing. We have been unable to locate their
Agda formalization for comparison with ours. The present development
replaces the ordinals by Brouwer codes with their natural order
throughout, so that no part of it is classical and function
extensionality is the only assumption. The natural order is sound
for the standard interpretation of the codes as ordinals under excluded
middle, by induction on a derivation of $a \le b$, the successor rule
using that the successor on ordinals is then monotone, so
\cref{thm:main} gives the classical statement as well. This remark is
not formalized.

They write that they do not claim their bound sharp, and leave open
whether the heights are cofinal in~$\eps$. \Cref{cor:least} answers
that question: they are, so $\eps$ is the least bound, and no tower
level bounds every term.

\begin{question}
\label{q:exact}
The exact height of a term as a function of its syntax is determined
by neither proof. Nor is the least~$n$ with
$\height(\dtree(t)) < \tower n$ for a given term~$t$, for which the
number $K(t)$ of~\cite{XiaoSun2026} is an upper bound.
\end{question}

\section{The formalization}
\label{sec:appendix}

The formalization was developed first, by LLM's, and the paper was
then unformalized from it, again by LLM's, each statement of the paper
being written from the Agda statement it points at. We describe the
files of the development, say where the main theorems are and what
has to be read by hand, describe the pointers that tie the paper to
the code, record how the paper was verified against it, and report the
independent review of the paper alone and the changes it led to.

\subsection{The files}
\label{sec:files}

The development is 28 Agda modules under the prefix
\AgdaModule{DialogueTreesHeight}, some three thousand lines of code, in
two directories, available at
\url{https://github.com/martinescardo/DialogueTreesHeight}. Every
module is checked with \texttt{--safe} and \texttt{--without-K}, with
no postulates and no holes, and every module but the index, which only
imports the others, takes function extensionality as a parameter named
\texttt{fe}. The library
it builds on is \texttt{TypeTopology}: the Brouwer codes come from
\AgdaModule{Ordinals.BrouwerCodes}, and the dialogue trees, the
combinatory system T and its dialogue interpretation from
\AgdaModule{EffectfulForcing.MFPSAndVariations}.

The directory \AgdaModule{BrouwerOrdinals}, 17 modules, holds the
ordinal arithmetic and the graded maps, and knows nothing of system T
or of dialogue trees. The modules \AgdaModule{Order},
\AgdaModule{Multiplication}, \AgdaModule{Epsilon0} and
\AgdaModule{MultClosure} are \cref{sec:codes}: the order, the
arithmetic, the tower and the closure properties of~$\eps$. The other
thirteen are \cref{sec:graded} together with the laws of absorption
of~\cref{lem:absorb}: \AgdaModule{MoveTuple} for the data,

\AgdaModule{RectOrbitAct} for application, abstraction and iteration
in~\cref{lem:calculus}, and \AgdaModule{Move}, \AgdaModule{MultAffine}
and \AgdaModule{Absorb} for the laws of absorption.

The directory \AgdaModule{DialogueTrees}, 10 modules, is the rest

are \cref{sec:majorant}; \AgdaModule{Predicate}, \AgdaModule{SClause},

\subsection{The pointers}
\label{sec:deformalizing}

Every numbered item carries a pointer, set in blue, to the Agda that
proves it. A pointer names a module and a definition in it, then
further names of the same module, and a name must be defined in the
module it is cited under rather than re-exported by it. A script
resolves every pointer against the sources and reports, for this
paper, 64 notes carrying 300 names, all resolving. Four numbered items
carry no pointer, three remarks and a question, which make no formal
claim, and \cref{lem:widths} carries a note in place of a lemma, its
two sides being definitionally equal. The paper was written statement
first, each Agda statement being read before the paper's was written,
and the proofs describe the route the Agda takes.

\subsection{The A/B/C verification}
\label{sec:abc}

The paper was then verified against the formalization in three
passes, the independence of the first two being the point of the
arrangement.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[A.]
  Read the paper alone, with the formalization closed, as a reader
  with no access to it: whether the mathematics is correct, whether
  each statement is self-contained, whether each proof proves what
  its statement says in steps that can be followed, and whether the
  prose says what it means. The findings go to a numbered list.
\item[B.]
  Audit the same text against the formalization, without opening the
  list from pass~A: each statement against the Agda it points at,
  each proof against the route the Agda takes, and each pointer
  against what exists and in which module.
\item[C.]
  Read the two lists together, merge the findings that coincide, drop
  a finding of~A that B refutes, and rank what survives with the
  errors first, the faithfulness gaps next and the language last.
  Each finding is then settled before the next is raised.
\end{enumerate}

On the first run pass~A produced sixteen findings and pass~B eleven, one
pair merged, and twenty-five survived into pass~C, of which five were
errors. They are worth naming as a sample of what each pass catches.
Pass~A found an operation used in the statement of a lemma and
nowhere defined, a constant used in four places and nowhere defined,
and a definition that reads as trivially the identity unless one is
told why it is not. Pass~B found a proof attributing two of its cases
to a lemma that neither case uses, a second proof describing a route
the Agda does not take, and a theorem stated in a shape weaker than
the one the pointer proves.

The procedure was run more than once, and with more than one model,
the later runs finding no error of mathematics and a small number of
faithfulness gaps and points of language, most of them in text the
first run had added. A limitation of every run should be recorded.
The value of pass~A lies in the reader's ignorance of the
formalization, and in each run the model carrying out that pass had
the formalization at hand, so that it could read the paper as it was
meant rather than as it stands. An independent pass~A is the single
change that would most strengthen the procedure.

The procedure was run once more after the four rounds
of~\cref{sec:review}, on the revised paper. It found no error of
mathematics and fourteen points: three of faithfulness, namely that the
soundness of the natural order for the standard interpretation of the
codes was asserted without saying that it is a classical remark and is
not formalized, that the paper said the development records what fails
without excluded middle when the classical modules had been discarded
in the pruning, and that it said every module takes function
extensionality as a parameter when the index does not; and eleven of
precision and language, most of them in the text the revision had
added. The same limitation applied, the model carrying out pass~A
having had the formalization at hand.

\subsection{The independent review}
\label{sec:review}

An independent pass~A was then carried out, on 28 October 2026. A copy
of the paper was made with the pointers, the links, this appendix and
every mention of the formalization removed, and it was given to an LLM
that had taken no part in the development, with the request to referee
it for mathematical correctness. The referee was asked not to consult
the internet, and it confirmed that it did not, which has to be taken
on trust. Its verdict was that the main claims were not established by
the paper as written, and it recommended a major revision. It found no
counterexample to the bound. It made three findings, and Mart\'in
Escard\'o, reading the same copy, made a fourth.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The reading of a tuple as a graded map was misstated. The paper
  took the cascade of output order~$j$ to be the whole suffix of the
  tuple from~$j$, which is longer than a cascade reading the input
  orders $j$ and above as soon as the output is wider than the input
  by more than one. The formalization reads the entries of orders $j$
  to~$p$ only, as~\cref{sec:rect} now says.
\item The four parts of~\cref{lem:calculus} were stated without proof,
  and the operations on graded maps that the clauses for~$\Sc$ and the
  recursor use were called routine and not defined. The laws of
  absorption of~\cref{lem:absorb}, the operations on cascades and on
  graded maps, with~\cref{lem:cascade-ops,lem:rect-ops}, and the proof
  of~\cref{lem:calculus} now supply them, and the proofs
  of~\cref{lem:s-clause,lem:iter-clause} now name the data they build.
\item The proof of~\cref{thm:lower} did not justify its limit step.
  The limit of the algebra applies a function to the tree
  $\beta\,\eta\,0$, and a function that ignores its argument makes no
  query. The relation asks about the limit only at Kleisli extensions,
  and the terms pass nothing else to a limit, but the paper said so in
  a single clause and gave neither the encodings nor the relation.
  Both are now written out, in~\cref{def:church,def:rel}, together
  with the preservation lemma, \cref{lem:rel}.
\item The generic oracle was defined only in passing, in the sentence
  after the definition of dialogue trees. It now has a definition of
  its own, \cref{def:kleisli-generic}, with the equations of the
  Kleisli extension it is built from.
\end{enumerate}

None of the four concerns the formalization, which reads tuples as
graded maps, proves the calculus, restricts the relation to Kleisli
extensions and defines the generic oracle as the paper now does, and
no Agda was changed. The first finding is a statement that does not
match the definition its pointer names, which pass~B of the earlier
runs had not caught. The second and third are proofs that the paper
had left to its pointers, which a reader of the paper alone cannot
follow.

A second copy, made in the same way from the revised paper, was then
given to the same referee, which again stated that it did not consult
the internet. Its verdict was that the revision resolves the three
findings of its first report, that it found no counterexample to the
main claims, and that the sharpness argument had become substantially
more convincing, and it recommended a minor revision for proof detail.
It asked for two additions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The elementary properties of the order and the laws of the
  arithmetic of codes in~\cref{sec:codes}, on which the closure
  of~$\eps$ rests, were stated without proof, and the proofs
  of~\cref{lem:tower} and~\cref{prop:closure} were sketches. The
  properties and the laws are now~\cref{lem:order,lem:add,lem:mult,lem:exp},
  with their proofs, the absorption of sums and products by the tower
  is~\cref{lem:tower-absorb}, and the proofs of~\cref{lem:tower}
  and~\cref{prop:closure} now give every step.
\item The proof of~\cref{prop:tree-of} excluded $\bS\,\eps \le \eps$
  ``by induction on the code''. The general fact is
  now~\cref{lem:irrefl}, with its proof.
\end{enumerate}

Again no Agda was changed. The second reading is less independent than
the first, since the referee had its own first report in view, but it
still had no access to the formalization.

A third copy, made in the same way, was given to the same referee,
which again stated that it did not consult the internet. It found no
remaining objection to the correctness of the main theorems and
recommended acceptance after minor clarifications, asking for three
proof details, none of which it regarded as a gap.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The inequality $\num n \btimes \omega^{e} \le \omega^{\bS e}$ in
  the proof of~\cref{lem:absorb} was proved ``by induction on~$e$''.
  The three cases are now written out, the limit case included.
\item The proof of~\cref{lem:dom-closure} called every part an
  induction on the type, and did not write out the ground case of the
  last part. The ground cases are now given, and the last part is
  derived from $a \le d_0 < \eps$ by the successor rule.
\item The induction in the proof of~\cref{prop:omega-attained} that the
  generic oracle raises a finite lower bound by one did not show the
  step at a query node. It is now an induction on the numeral, and the
  step inverts a bound on a limit to a bound on one of its subtrees.
\end{enumerate}

No Agda was changed in this round either.

A fourth copy, made in the same way, was given to the same referee,
which again stated that it did not consult the internet. It confirmed
that the three clarifications were present, rechecked how the bound on
the iterates feeds the clause for the recursor and how the proof of the
lower bound tests the limit of the algebra on Kleisli extensions, found
no new gap, and recommended acceptance, with no remaining objection to
the correctness of the paper. It added that its assessment is of the
written proof, not a machine-checked verification. No change was asked
for, and none was made.
